# T27 Complete your NZ Traveller Declaration — research note

## Questions

- Who must complete a New Zealand Traveller Declaration (NZTD), when, where, and what does it cost?
- What does it ask for, and can it be changed or done on paper?
- What must you declare or answer "Yes" to on arrival (goods, cash, medicine, biosecurity risk items)?
- What is the biosecurity fine for a false or missing declaration?
- What do official pages say about prescription, controlled and herbal medicines?
- Are fee-charging declaration sites official?

## Uncertainties

- **Is the NZ$800 tier in force?** The Beehive release (29 October 2025) announces "a two-tiered infringement approach" with $800 for high-risk goods and says "We hope to see a draft bill incorporating the changes progress through Parliament later next year." Every in-force Customs, MPI and NZTD page opened says NZ$400 (NZTD says "minimum"). What would settle it: an MPI or Customs page, or legislation.govt.nz, showing a commencement date.
- **Herbal medicines: declare or not?** Medsafe (2021) says herbal medicines "may be carried with you without the above documentation". Customs' medicines page lists "traditional/herbal medicines" as items to declare to MPI. Both are recorded; no side is taken. What would settle it: an updated Medsafe, Customs or MPI page that addresses both rules.
- **Duty and GST above NZ$700.** The on-arrival page gives NZ$700 as a declare threshold but not what is charged. What would settle it: Customs' import allowances and charges page (not opened).
- **Medsafe page age.** It is dated 2021 and refers to an "arrival card". What would settle it: a current Medsafe revision.
- **NZTD page date.** The "28th October 2025" update date is from the video section; the page itself carries no other date.

## Findings

**Who, when, cost.** "It is FREE and everyone travelling into New Zealand must complete a declaration, including New Zealand passport holders." "A declaration needs to be completed for each traveller, including babies and children." "Caregivers may complete a declaration on behalf of children or babies they are travelling with." NZ visa or NZeTA holders "must complete an NZTD as well" (https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/). For air travellers "the earliest you can submit your declaration is 24 hours before you start your trip to New Zealand", and it "needs to be submitted by the time you reach passport control" (https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/completing-your-declaration/). With a stopover where you leave the airport with your bags, the earliest is "24 hours before your flight leaves your stopover location for New Zealand" (same URL). Transit passengers who stay in the transit area "do not need to complete a declaration" (same URL).

**What it asks, changes, paper.** It asks for "Passport details; Contact details in New Zealand (if staying in multiple locations, use the first address you will be at in New Zealand); Travel history from the last 30 days; Flight or voyage details; Information about what you are bringing into New Zealand, including checked-in luggage and carry-on bags; Immigration status including your visa or NZeTA, if you need one." With several passports: "Enter the details of the passport you will use to enter New Zealand." "You will need to resubmit your declaration if you make any changes to it." "If you make a change to your flight booking, you need to update your digital declaration." You are emailed a reference number; "You do not need to print anything out." "There is a paper declaration form available on arrival, if needed." It is "available in multiple languages. Answers must be in English." (all https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/completing-your-declaration/). The app "may not be available in some international regions that use app/app-store blocking" (https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/nztd-app/).

**Scam sites.** "Third-party sites charging a fee for completing a traveller declaration are not affiliated with the New Zealand Government. If you are asked for payment, please do not proceed." (Customs notice URL above).

**What to declare.** Customs lists: "medicine – over three months' supply, or medicine not prescribed to you"; "restricted or prohibited goods"; "alcohol – more than three bottles of spirits (not exceeding 1.125 litres each) and 4.5 litres of wine or beer"; "tobacco more than 50 cigarettes, or 50 grams of tobacco products"; "goods obtained overseas and/or purchased duty-free in New Zealand with a total value of more than NZ$700 (including gifts)"; "goods carried for business or commercial use"; "goods carried on behalf of another person"; "cash – NZ$10,000 or more (or foreign equivalent)" (https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/travelling-to-new-zealand/on-your-arrival). "You must: answer all questions on your New Zealand Traveller Declaration (NZTD) / produce your identity documents. It is an offence not to do so." Clothing, footwear, jewellery and toiletries are not required to be declared if they meet biosecurity requirements and are "intended solely for your own use". "It's always better to declare items you're unsure about", and "You will find marked amnesty bins at all New Zealand international airports." (same URL).

**Biosecurity.** MPI: "declare all risk items – like food, plants, wooden products, soil, water, outdoor equipment, and animal products". "All travellers into New Zealand must complete a declaration even if you don’t have anything to declare." Risk examples include "Any food – cooked, uncooked, fresh, preserved, packaged or dried" and used "footwear, tents, camping, hunting, hiking, golf or sports equipment". "If in doubt, ask a quarantine officer when you arrive at the airport." (https://www.mpi.govt.nz/bring-send-to-nz/bringing-and-posting-items-to-nz/how-to-declare-items-when-arriving-in-nz). Customs: "MPI officers will inspect your items ... They may need to treat items to be able to return them to you. You will have to pay for this." (https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/medicines).

**The fine.** In-force pages say NZ$400 (quotes under Sources used). MPI: reasons such as "by accident" or "because you forgot" are no excuse; "In legal terms, it's called a strict liability offence." Options once fined: pay immediately, within 14 days, request a waiver, or request a hearing (https://www.mpi.govt.nz/bring-send-to-nz/bringing-and-posting-items-to-nz/what-happens-if-you-fail-to-declare). The announced NZ$800 tier is under Uncertainties.

**Medicines.** Customs: for prescription medicine "bring a copy of your prescription, or a letter from your doctor confirming you are being treated with the medicine / keep the medicine in its original packaging / bring no more than three months of supply." You must declare it if "you are carrying more than three months of supply" or "the medicine is not prescribed to you or a family member". Controlled drugs: declare them on the NZTD, original packaging, "no more than one month of supply", plus a prescription copy or doctor's letter. "All medicines containing pseudoephedrine are controlled drugs"; keep them in hand luggage (https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/medicines). Herbal medicines: Customs and Medsafe pages differ (quotes under Sources used).

**Support.** Customs Contact Centre, 24/7: 0800 359 269 (NZ toll free), +64 4 931 5799 (international) (https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/completing-your-declaration/).

### Candidate lines

**why**
- Everyone entering New Zealand completes a declaration, including NZ passport holders, babies and children. https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/
- Customs and MPI use it to check goods and biosecurity risk items before you leave the arrival hall. https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/travelling-to-new-zealand/on-your-arrival

**steps**
- Open the official site or the NZTD app to start your declaration. https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/
- Submit it from 24 hours before your trip starts (air travel), and before you reach passport control. https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/completing-your-declaration/ — fact: 24 hours; "the earliest you can submit your declaration is 24 hours before you start your trip to New Zealand"; that URL; B.
- Have ready your passport details, your first NZ address, 30 days of travel history, flight details and visa or NZeTA. https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/completing-your-declaration/ — fact: 30 days; "Travel history from the last 30 days"; that URL; B.
- Enter the passport you will use to enter New Zealand if you hold more than one. https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/completing-your-declaration/
- Answer "Yes" for alcohol or tobacco above Customs' limits and for goods worth more than NZ$700 (gifts included). https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/travelling-to-new-zealand/on-your-arrival — facts: NZ$700; "goods obtained overseas and/or purchased duty-free in New Zealand with a total value of more than NZ$700 (including gifts)"; that URL; A.
- Answer "Yes" for cash of NZ$10,000 or more, goods for business, and goods carried for another person. https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/travelling-to-new-zealand/on-your-arrival — fact: NZ$10,000; "cash – NZ$10,000 or more (or foreign equivalent)"; that URL; A.
- Declare food, plants, wooden items, soil, used outdoor gear and animal products. https://www.mpi.govt.nz/bring-send-to-nz/bringing-and-posting-items-to-nz/how-to-declare-items-when-arriving-in-nz
- Declare medicine that is over three months' supply or not prescribed to you. https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/travelling-to-new-zealand/on-your-arrival — fact: three months; "medicine – over three months' supply, or medicine not prescribed to you"; that URL; B.
- Update and resubmit the declaration if your details or flight change. https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/completing-your-declaration/
- Use the amnesty bins in the airport, or ask a quarantine officer, for items you are unsure about. https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/travelling-to-new-zealand/on-your-arrival

**documents**
- Keep your emailed reference number; you do not need to print anything. https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/completing-your-declaration/
- For prescription medicine, Customs asks for a prescription copy or a doctor's letter, and the original packaging. https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/medicines

**mistakes**
- Paying a third-party site to complete it; Customs says such sites are not affiliated with the NZ Government. https://www.customs.govt.nz/about-us/news/important-notices/use-the-official-government-website-for-completing-the-new-zealand-traveller-declaration
- Skipping it because you hold an NZ passport or have nothing to declare; MPI says all travellers complete one. https://www.mpi.govt.nz/bring-send-to-nz/bringing-and-posting-items-to-nz/how-to-declare-items-when-arriving-in-nz
- Leaving out an item by accident; MPI says "because you forgot" is no excuse. https://www.mpi.govt.nz/bring-send-to-nz/bringing-and-posting-items-to-nz/what-happens-if-you-fail-to-declare

**watchOuts**
- The in-force Customs and MPI pages state an instant fine of NZ$400 for a false or missing declaration. https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/travelling-to-new-zealand/on-your-arrival — fact: NZ$400; "You could face a $400 instant fine if: you fail to declare restricted or prohibited items / you make a false or incorrect declaration in your New Zealand Traveller Declaration."; that URL; A.
- The NZTD site calls NZ$400 a "minimum" instant fine and lists prosecution and deportation as possible penalties. https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/completing-your-declaration/
- If the app is blocked in your region, the online form is the alternative; a paper form is available on arrival. https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/nztd-app/
- Customs says controlled drugs are limited to one month of supply and must be declared. https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/medicines — fact: one month; "bring no more than one month of supply"; that URL; B.
- Medsafe says herbal medicines "may be carried with you without the above documentation"; Customs lists "traditional/herbal medicines" as items to declare. Read both pages. https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/consumers/MIET/ImportMedicines.asp and https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/medicines
- Transit passengers who stay in the airport transit area do not need to complete one. https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/completing-your-declaration/

**cost**
- The declaration is free. https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/ — fact: free; "It is FREE and everyone travelling into New Zealand must complete a declaration"; that URL; A.
- If MPI must treat an item to return it to you, you pay for the treatment. https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/medicines

**advice**
- For help with the form, the Customs Contact Centre is open 24/7 on 0800 359 269 (NZ) or +64 4 931 5799 (international). https://www.travellerdeclaration.govt.nz/completing-your-declaration/
