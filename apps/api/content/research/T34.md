# T34 Understand your NZ tax residency and foreign-income exemption — research note

## Questions

- How does Inland Revenue say tax residency is decided (183 days, permanent place of abode, 325 days)?
- Are NZ tax residents taxed on worldwide income?
- What is the temporary tax exemption: length, who qualifies, automatic or opt-out, what is and is not covered?
- What does IRD say about double tax agreements, advice, and foreign investment funds for new migrants?

## Findings

**Residency.** "You become a New Zealand tax resident when the first of these happens: you've been in New Zealand for more than 183 days in any 12-month period (unless you're a non-resident visitor); you have a permanent place of abode in New Zealand." Parts of days count as whole days, days need not be consecutive, status is "backdated to the first of the 183 days". Tax residency "is different from your immigration status." (residency page, updated 25 Jun 2026).

**Abode.** "somewhere, such as a house, where you normally live in New Zealand even if you are not there all the time"; need not own it; ties listed (return frequency, time, family and social, economic interests, employment, intention to return).

**Leaving.** Non-resident taxpayer only if no permanent place of abode and "away from New Zealand for more than 325 days in any 12-month period"; parts of days in NZ do not count as whole days absent; backdated to first of the 325 days.

**Worldwide income.** "As a New Zealand tax resident, you'll generally pay tax on your worldwide income", even if not brought into NZ or tax was deducted abroad; "usually be eligible for a foreign tax credit" (international page).

**Exemption.** "The exemption lasts for about 4 years after you become a New Zealand tax resident." Automatic, once only. Qualify if new migrant or returning New Zealander, tax resident on or after 1 April 2006, and not resident in the 10 years before. Ends early if you or a transitional-resident partner apply for Working for Families (incl. Best Start); include exempt income in your IR3 ("You cannot revoke the choice to 'opt out'"); confirm to a foreign jurisdiction and IRD that you'll pay NZ tax on overseas income; or tell IRD you no longer want it. Covers "overseas interest, dividends, foreign investment fund income, rent". Not exempt: "Income you earn overseas from employment or providing personal services"; "full or partial relief ... under an applicable double tax agreement" may be available. End date: earlier of 4 years after end of the month you qualify by 183 days or abode; end is not backdated. After: "subject to New Zealand income tax on your worldwide income". Advice: "You may wish to speak with a tax professional". Flowchart IR1249 linked.

**FIF.** International page: holdings over $50,000 probably need FIF calculation (fair dividend rate most common); with the exemption "you do not ... apply the FIF rules"; read IR461 or consult a tax professional.
