## Findings

**Concession criteria (Customs lists them; this is not an assessment).** Customs: "You might qualify for “household effects” concessions if: you’re moving to New Zealand for the first time / you're moving back after 21 months or more away." It adds: "If you qualify for household concessions, this means you won’t have to pay GST and duties on most of your items." and "If you do not qualify for a concession, duty and GST will apply." (https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/move-to-new-zealand-permanently/household-effects)

**Document that authorises residence.** Customs: "You must hold a document authorising residence in New Zealand. This can be one of the following:" and lists a NZ passport, an Australian passport, "a current New Zealand residence visa or permit, or a current returning resident’s visa or permit", "a current work visa or work permit that was issued for a minimum of 12 months", and "a current visitor’s visa or permit that was issued for a minimum of 3 years (excludes a visitor's visa that allows multiple visits up to six months per visit)". It also says "Student visa holders do not qualify. However, you may bring items necessary for you to complete your studies (for example, a desk, text books or a computer)." (same Customs URL)

**Other criteria on the page.** "You must have resided or been domiciled outside of New Zealand for the whole of the 21-month period preceding your arrival (except for brief holidays or business trips to New Zealand)." "Your household effects must have been personally owned and used by you before you depart for New Zealand." "Your household effects must be for your own use and not be intended for any other person, for gift, or for exchange." (same Customs URL)

**New and unused items.** "If the effects are brand new/ unused, you must declare them, because duty and GST will apply." Items not covered include "new and unused items / cars / scooters / motorbikes / boats / aircraft / commercial items". (same Customs URL)

**Timing.** Customs sets no arrival deadline except: "If goods falling within the description and criteria listed above are imported into New Zealand more than five years after the arrival of the passenger into New Zealand, the goods may be admitted under this concession only with the consent of a Customs officer." On personal effects (a separate page): "Note: You do not have to be in New Zealand when your shipment arrives." (https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/move-to-new-zealand-permanently/wearing-apparel-and-personal-effects/)

**Process and documents.** Step 1: "You should choose who will complete Customs clearance: the shipping company (if they offer this service / a Customs broker of your choice / complete Customs clearance yourself." Documents named: "NZCS 218: Bring Personal Household Items into New Zealand (PDF, 393 KB)"; "Copy of your passport (photo ID)"; "Document authorising residence in New Zealand (only if you don’t hold a New Zealand or Australian passport)"; "Packing list (a list of all the household effects you are shipping to New Zealand)"; a shipping document (airway bill, bill of lading, arrival notice, consignment note, or NZ Post notice); "Any permits that may be required for Prohibited and restricted items"; "Any other relevant supporting documents, such as receipts for new items"; "MPI Supplementary Biosecurity Declaration (PDF, 865 KB)". "Customs may examine your shipment to verify any of the details you have provided. This may delay your clearance." If an amount is payable: "You will be required to pay this amount online prior to the release of your shipment." (household effects URL)

**Personal effects are a separate Customs page.** It lists clothing, jewellery, toiletries, medical devices, laptops and phones, and "Personally owned medications, including prescription medicines (up to three months’ supply)". "Controlled drugs must travel with you and cannot be sent as unaccompanied baggage." (wearing-apparel URL above)

**MPI on shipped goods.** "Shipments of personal items must be accompanied by a detailed inventory listing all of the items in the consignment, and you must declare any restricted items." "In most cases, no inspection of cargo is necessary – but inspection of sea containers is mandatory." "Inspections are carried out in MPI approved warehouses called transitional facilities." For full containers "you'll need to meet extra requirements, including completing a quarantine declaration." Wood: "If your goods are shipped to New Zealand in wooden crates or containers, or wood has been used as flooring or walls, you'll need to comply with requirements for importing wood packaging." On the supplementary declaration: "This voluntary declaration provides more information about specific items and could change the risk status of your consignment." Declared prohibited items may be "inspected and returned to you / re-shipped / destroyed at an approved facility / treated at an approved facility ... Be aware that some items cannot be treated. Fees and charges will apply." Contact: info@mpi.govt.nz. (https://www.mpi.govt.nz/bring-send-to-nz/clearance-of-personal-goods-and-mail-to-nz/household-goods-and-personal-effects-importing-to-nz)

**MPI inspection fees.** "MPI charges for costs related to inspecting unaccompanied household goods and personal items brought into New Zealand by air or sea. MPI may bill you for these services, or you may be charged by your import or shipping agent." Scale "based on the number of items that need inspecting": $38.88 for 1 item; $77.74 for 2 to 4; $155.50 for 5 to 12; $202.15 for 13 to 20; $241.03 for 21 to 28; $279.89 for 29 to 36; "$38.88 for each set of items (up to 8 items) beyond 36 items." GST treatment is not stated. (https://www.mpi.govt.nz/bring-send-to-nz/clearance-of-personal-goods-and-mail-to-nz/fees-and-charges-for-clearance-of-goods)

**Goods levy.** Customs: "You will have to pay a Goods Levy for each consignment of personal and household goods imported unaccompanied, for example by air or sea freight. The amount of the levy is dependent on the value of the consignment and the mode of transport (air, sea, mail)." On personal effects: "Your broker or freight forwarder pays these levies and may pass them on to you." Levies changed on 1 April 2026 (goods-levies-2026-changes URL). Quick reference PDF, all excl. GST: high-value import (over NZD$1,000) air and mail, Customs $7.24 + MPI $44.57 = $51.81; high-value sea, $73.87 + $44.57 = $118.44; low-value (NZD$1,000 or less) air, $1.46 + $0.75 = $2.21; low-value sea, $1.34 + $0.75 = $2.09 (https://www.customs.govt.nz/media/utdnkqm0/quick-reference-guide-all-goods-levies-from-1-april-2026.pdf).

**Vehicles.** Customs states household concessions cannot be claimed for cars, scooters or motorbikes, and covers them on its vehicles page (URL in Sources used).

### Candidate lines

**why**
- Customs lists criteria for a household effects concession; if they are met, it says you won't pay GST and duties on most items, and otherwise duty and GST apply. https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/move-to-new-zealand-permanently/household-effects
- MPI inspects and charges for unaccompanied household goods, so what you ship affects cost and clearance time. https://www.mpi.govt.nz/bring-send-to-nz/clearance-of-personal-goods-and-mail-to-nz/fees-and-charges-for-clearance-of-goods

**steps**
1. Open Customs' household effects page and read the criteria and clearance steps. https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/move-to-new-zealand-permanently/household-effects
2. Check the criteria Customs lists: you might qualify if you are moving to New Zealand for the first time or moving back after 21 months or more away. Check the other listed criteria on that page. Same URL.
3. Choose who clears the shipment: the shipping company (if they offer it), a Customs broker, or you. Same URL.
4. Prepare a packing list of all the household effects you are shipping. Same URL.
5. Make a detailed inventory and number your boxes. MPI says a good tip is to number boxes and list the items inside. https://www.mpi.govt.nz/bring-send-to-nz/clearance-of-personal-goods-and-mail-to-nz/household-goods-and-personal-effects-importing-to-nz
6. Complete form NZCS 218 and gather the other documents Customs names. Same Customs URL as step 1.
7. Consider the MPI supplementary declaration if goods may be a biosecurity risk. MPI says it is voluntary and may lead to reduced clearance costs and faster clearance times. MPI household goods URL.
8. If you use wooden crates, or wood is part of your goods, check MPI's wood packaging requirements. MPI household goods URL.
9. Pay any amount Customs advises online before the shipment is released. Customs household effects URL.
10. For cars, scooters or motorbikes, use Customs' vehicles page instead. https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/move-to-new-zealand-permanently/vehicles-vessels-and-aircraft/

**documents**
- Customs lists: NZCS 218, a copy of your passport, a packing list, a shipping document (airway bill, bill of lading, arrival notice, consignment note or NZ Post notice) and the MPI Supplementary Biosecurity Declaration. Customs household effects URL.
- Customs lists a document authorising residence if you don't hold a New Zealand or Australian passport; it names the accepted documents on its page. Same URL.
- Customs also lists any permits for prohibited and restricted items, and receipts for new items. Same URL.

**mistakes**
- Shipping new or unused items: Customs says they must be declared and duty and GST apply. Customs household effects URL.
- Expecting the concession to cover cars, scooters, motorbikes, boats, aircraft or commercial items: Customs says it cannot be claimed for them. Same URL.
- Sending incomplete paperwork: Customs says it will not prepare or complete a clearance until it has all documents. Same URL.
- Including items for a gift, exchange or another person: Customs says effects must be for your own use. Same URL.

**watchOuts**
- Customs says student visa holders do not qualify for the concession, but may bring items needed to complete their studies. Customs household effects URL.
- Customs may examine your shipment, which "may delay your clearance". Same URL.
- MPI says inspection of sea containers is mandatory and happens in an MPI approved transitional facility. MPI household goods URL.
- MPI says declared prohibited items may be returned, re-shipped, destroyed or treated, with fees and charges. MPI household goods URL.
- Customs says goods arriving more than five years after you arrive may be admitted under the concession only with a Customs officer's consent. Customs household effects URL.
- Customs says you don't have to be in New Zealand when a shipment of personal effects arrives. https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/move-to-new-zealand-permanently/wearing-apparel-and-personal-effects/

**cost**
- Customs says a Goods Levy applies to each unaccompanied consignment, depending on its value and mode of transport. Customs household effects URL.
- Goods levy from 1 April 2026, excl. GST: over NZD$1,000 by air $51.81, by sea $118.44; NZD$1,000 or less by air $2.21, by sea $2.09. Customs' page doesn't say which tier a household shipment falls under. https://www.customs.govt.nz/media/utdnkqm0/quick-reference-guide-all-goods-levies-from-1-april-2026.pdf

- MPI charges $38.88 for 1 item inspected, rising to $279.89 for 29 to 36 items, and $38.88 per further set of up to 8 items. MPI fees page does not say whether GST is included. https://www.mpi.govt.nz/bring-send-to-nz/clearance-of-personal-goods-and-mail-to-nz/fees-and-charges-for-clearance-of-goods
