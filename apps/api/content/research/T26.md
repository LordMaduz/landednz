## Questions

- Which MPI standard applies in 2026, and how do country categories 1, 2 and 3 change the steps?

- How do the import permit and quarantine work, and who pays?
- Which dogs cannot be imported, and what about hybrids?
- What must you do with the council for a dog after arrival (registration, microchip)?

## Findings

**Which standard.** MPI's guide: "This guidance covers requirements set out in an updated import health standard that came into force on 1 July 2026" and "There is a 9-month transition period when you can still import cats and dogs under the requirements of the previous import health standard issued in 2021" (MPI step-by-step 2026 URL above; page "Last reviewed: 22.10.26"). The IHS (dated 12 May 2026) says: "This standard revokes and replaces the Import Health Standard: Cats and Dogs, dated 7 April 2021" (IHS URL above). The two pages are quoted as they stand and not reconciled. Neither opened page gives an end date.

**Categories.** "Category 1: Australia (including Norfolk Island and the Cocos/Keeling Islands). Category 2: Rabies-free countries or territories. Category 3: Countries or territories where rabies is absent or well controlled." "Cats and dogs can be imported only from categorised countries." The list is in Schedule 9 of Specified Approvals (MPI guide). MPI's 10 steps run from "Read the import health standard" to "Notify MPI before arrival, if needed" (MPI guide).

**Timed chain.**
- Identification: "must be permanently and uniquely identified with a microchip"; ISO 11784/11785 chips "are required for dog registration in New Zealand" (IHS 1.5, guidance).
- Pre-export ID check: "This must be done at least 6 months before bringing your pet to New Zealand." (MPI guide). IHS 1.11(3), category 2 cats: microchip "scanned and verified by an official veterinarian in the country of export at least 6 months prior to shipment".
- Category 2 residency: "for at least 6 months before shipment to, or arrival in, New Zealand, (unless the rabies import requirements have been met), or since birth, or since direct importation from New Zealand." (MPI guide).
- Category 3 rabies (IHS 2.1.3): vaccinated "when the animal was at least 12 weeks of age"; "In the case of a primary vaccination, the vaccine must be given not less than 6 months before the date of shipment"; titre (RNATT) sample "not less than 3 months and not more than 12 months before the date of shipment, with a result of at least 0.5 IU/mL". Guidance: "wait 3-4 weeks after the rabies vaccination ... before taking a sample for the rabies titration test."
- Last days (IHS 1.12, 2.1.1, 2.2): vet inspection "in the 2 days before the date of shipment"; cat tick and flea treatment "in the 16 days before the date of shipment"; dogs treated twice, last "in the 16 days before the date of shipment".
- Pregnancy: "Cats and dogs must not be more than 42 days pregnant on the date of shipment." Air arrivals: "can arrive only at Auckland or Christchurch airports." (MPI guide).
- Non-compliant testing "will be repeated in New Zealand, which will result in additional costs to the importer, and is likely to extend the quarantine period." (IHS 1.7).

**Permit and quarantine.** "Applications for an import permit can only be done online." "Ensure you apply for the import permit at least 30 working days before the date you need it." "Once your cat or dog is booked into a quarantine facility, you can apply for a permit" (MPI guide). "All cats and dogs (except those from Australia and assistance dogs) are required to have a minimum of 10 days quarantine at an MPI-approved quarantine facility. A booking confirmation letter needs to accompany your application for an import permit" (MPI guide). IHS 1.15: "remain in quarantine for a minimum period of 10 days". Costs: "All costs involved with documentation, transport, storage and obtaining a biosecurity clearance must be covered by the importer or agent." (IHS). MPI contact: animal.imports@mpi.govt.nz. MPI says "We recommend you use a pet transport company" (note: paraphrase of the MPI guide, no firm named).

**Documents (IHS 1.13).** Category 2: "A signed quarantine booking form". Category 3: rabies vaccination records, RNATT results, the Category 3 Rabies Certification Form and a signed quarantine booking form. Medication: a declaration of medicines form plus prescription; "Generally, up to a 3-month supply will be given clearance." (MPI guide).

**Australia (category 1).** "a permit is not required if your cat or dog is coming from Australia, Norfolk Island, or Cocos (Keeling) Islands"; notify MPI "at least 5 working days before the scheduled arrival time" (MPI guide). Sea arrivals need a permit and quarantine (IHS 1.13(1)(b)).

**Not allowed.** Dog Control Act s30A(1) bars importing dogs of the Schedule 4 breeds or types: Brazilian Fila, Dogo Argentino, Japanese Tosa, Perro de Presa Canario, American Pit Bull Terrier (Act URL above; Fila read from the MPI page listing the same five). "A hybrid cat or dog (other than a Bengal cat) is likely to be considered a new organism. An approval is required by the Environmental Protection Authority (EPA)" (MPI guide).

**Council dog registration.** s36(1): the owner must apply "to the territorial authority in whose district the dog is ordinarily kept"; s36(3): for an acquired unregistered dog, "forthwith or before the day on which the dog attains the age of 3 months, whichever is the later" (Act URL above). s42(1): fine "not exceeding $3,000" for an unregistered dog over 3 months. s36A: a dog "registered for the first time on or after 1 July 2006" must be implanted with a microchip transponder. Auckland Council: registration "runs from 1 July to 30 June the following year", cost "will depend on when you register your dog and whether your dog is desexed", it asks for "microchip number and vet certificates", disc or strap "within 10 working days", and "There is an infringement notice of $300 if you do not register your dog." (Auckland URL above).

### Candidate lines

**why**
- Rules and timing depend on the country your pet comes from, so check its category first. https://www.mpi.govt.nz/bring-send-to-nz/pets-travelling-to-nz/bringing-cats-and-dogs-to-nz/bringing-cats-and-dogs-to-nz-using-the-2026-import-health-standard/step-by-step-guide-to-bringing-cats-and-dogs-to-nz-2026
- MPI's guide says the 2026 standard came into force on 1 July 2026 and that a 9-month transition still allows the 2021 requirements; the IHS says it revokes the 2021 standard. Check which one MPI tells you applies. (same MPI guide URL; IHS URL)

**steps**
1. Open MPI's step-by-step guide for cats and dogs, check your country's category and read the import health standard. (MPI guide URL)
2. Have the pet microchipped with an ISO 11784/11785 chip; the number is checked at every exam, test and vaccine. https://www.mpi.govt.nz/dmsdocument/71909-Cats-and-Dogs-2026-Import-Health-Standard
3. Book the pre-export identification check at least 6 months before travel. (MPI guide URL) [fact: 6 months; "at least 6 months before bringing your pet to New Zealand"; MPI guide URL; B]
4. Category 2: MPI sets residency of at least 6 months before shipment or arrival, unless the rabies requirements were met. (MPI guide URL) [fact: 6 months; "for at least 6 months before shipment to, or arrival in, New Zealand"; MPI guide URL; B]
5. Category 3: the IHS requires the primary rabies vaccine not less than 6 months before shipment, then a titre sample 3 to 12 months before shipment, taken 3 to 4 weeks after the vaccine. (IHS URL) [fact: 6 months, 3 to 12 months, 3-4 weeks; quotes in Findings; IHS URL; B]
6. Book an MPI-approved quarantine facility; the permit application needs its booking confirmation. (MPI guide URL)
7. Apply for the import permit online at least 30 working days before you need it. (MPI guide URL) [fact: 30 working days; "at least 30 working days before the date you need it"; MPI guide URL; B]
8. If the pet takes medication, declare it with the MPI form and a prescription. (MPI guide URL)
9. Arrange tick and flea treatment in the 16 days before shipment and a vet inspection in the 2 days before shipment. (IHS URL) [fact: 16 days, 2 days; "in the 16 days before the date of shipment", "in the 2 days before the date of shipment"; IHS URL; B]
10. From Australia, no permit is needed; email MPI at least 5 working days before arrival. (MPI guide URL) [fact: 5 working days; "at least 5 working days before the scheduled arrival time"; MPI guide URL; B]
11. Arrive at Auckland or Christchurch airport; categories 2 and 3 go to quarantine for at least 10 days. (MPI guide URL) [fact: 10 days; "a minimum of 10 days quarantine"; MPI guide URL; B]
12. Dogs: register with the council for the district where the dog is kept once it arrives, with the microchip number and vet certificates. https://www.aucklandcouncil.govt.nz/en/dogs-animals/register-your-dog/register-dog-first-time.html

**documents**
- Category 2: a signed quarantine booking form. Category 3: rabies certificates, RNATT results, the Rabies Certification Form and the booking form. (IHS URL)
- A quarantine booking confirmation letter goes with the permit application. (MPI guide URL)
- For council registration: microchip number and vet certificates. (Auckland URL)

**mistakes**
- Leaving the 6-month items late; the ID check, residency and rabies vaccine timings are all measured back from shipment. (MPI guide URL; IHS URL)
- Applying for the permit before a quarantine booking exists; MPI says to apply once booked. (MPI guide URL)
- Testing that does not comply is repeated in New Zealand at the importer's cost and may extend quarantine. (IHS URL)

**watchOuts**
- Five dog breeds or types are banned from import by law, and hybrid cats and dogs (other than Bengal cats) need EPA approval. https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1996/0013/latest/whole.html ; (MPI guide URL)
- Cats and dogs must not be more than 42 days pregnant on the date of shipment. (MPI guide URL) [fact: 42 days; "must not be more than 42 days pregnant on the date of shipment"; MPI guide URL; B]
- Dog registration runs 1 July to 30 June; Auckland Council lists a $300 infringement notice for an unregistered dog. (Auckland URL) [fact: $300; "There is an infringement notice of $300 if you do not register your dog."; Auckland URL; A]
- The Dog Control Act sets a fine of up to $3,000 for an unregistered dog over 3 months. (Act URL) [fact: $3,000; "not exceeding $3,000"; Act URL; A]

**cost**
- You pay all costs of documents, transport, storage and biosecurity clearance. (IHS URL)
- Council registration cost depends on when you register and whether the dog is desexed. (Auckland URL)

**advice**
- MPI recommends using a pet transport company; questions go to animal.imports@mpi.govt.nz. (MPI guide URL)
