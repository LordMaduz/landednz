## Uncertainties

- **INZ and NZTA disagree on the overseas-licence limit.** INZ Getting around says "for the first 18 months you are here" with no mention of a change. NZTA says 18 months if the last arrival is before 1 November 2026 and 12 months if on or after, counted "from your date of arrival", and that leaving and returning restarts the clock. INZ also counts from "you are here"; NZTA from last arrival. What would settle it: INZ updating its page, or INZ/NZTA confirming which page is current.
- **NZTA's "partway through conversion" sentence still says 18 months.** It says that if you have started converting but have no NZ licence yet, you can drive on your overseas licence "for up to 18 months from when you last arrived". It is unclear whether this holds after 31 October 2026. What would settle it: NZTA confirming or updating the wording. Left out of candidate lines.
- **Arrive before 1 November 2026, then leave and re-enter on or after it.** By NZTA's quotes the last arrival decides, which would give 12 months, but NZTA does not state this case. What would settle it: NZTA stating it.
- **Motorcycle and truck limits.** Not opened; not stated.
- **Home-country watch-out has no direct official source.** No allowed page tells newcomers to check the rules of the country they are leaving. The closest backed wording is IRD's "consult a tax professional if you're in doubt" plus INZ's double-taxation line. Neither names the home country. Proposed wording (backed as far as it goes): "International tax can be complex. IRD says you may want to consult a tax professional if you're in doubt about your situation." The owner decides whether that is enough, or whether the wording "check your own country's rules with a professional there" is kept as unsourced general advice. What would settle it: an allowed page that says this directly (for example the IR1096 text, not retrieved).
- **School reports and overseas immunisation recording.** No allowed raw page confirms either; see Deliberately not said.
- **Truncated govt.nz enrolment quote.** The line on free enrolment for domestic students was cut by text extraction and is not used. What would settle it: re-reading the live page.
- **NZTA pages need a real browser.** Curl returns a bot-protection stub; the quotes come from browser page text. Re-check before publishing.

## Findings

**What to bring.** INZ lists documents it says are useful to have in NZ: "medical records, such as doctor or dental records and vaccination certificates birth certificates marriage certificates academic qualifications references from previous employers and landlords your CV (curriculum vitae/resume) credit references international driver licences or permits". It adds "Try to have the original or a certified copy of these documents." The wording is "useful to have", not a legal requirement (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/moving-to-new-zealand/planning-your-move-to-new-zealand/). Same page: "Use the NZ Ready tool to help plan your move to New Zealand. It can give you a list of things to do for your situation." (NZ Ready: https://www.immigration.govt.nz/nz-ready/, not opened, content not described.) Visa documents are separate: "When you decide on a visa, check what documents you will need to provide." (same planning page).

**Driving on an overseas licence.** NZTA: "From 1 November 2026, the time you can drive on an overseas car licence in New Zealand is changing." "If your last date of arrival in New Zealand is before 1 November 2026, you can drive on your overseas car licence for 18 months from your date of arrival." "If your last date of arrival in New Zealand is on or after 1 November 2026, you can drive on your overseas car licence for 12 months from your date of arrival." At the end you need to "convert your overseas licence to a New Zealand licence, or stop driving." "If you leave New Zealand and come back, your 12 months will start again." "Allow plenty of time to convert your licence. It may take a while to get a test booking." Background: "In 2024, the government extended the amount of time you could drive on your overseas licence from 12 to 18 months." "The extension was put in place for 2 years and will end on 31 October 2026." (https://www.nzta.govt.nz/travelling-on-our-roads/visitors-and-new-residents/driving-on-nz-roads/time-limit-extended-for-driving-on-an-overseas-licence). Conditions: the licence "must be current and valid", with no NZ disqualification or suspension (same URL). Conversion page: "Your overseas licence is valid if: it's either current or expired within the last 12 months; it isn't suspended, disqualified or revoked in the country it was issued in." and "You must present your physical overseas driver licence." (https://www.nzta.govt.nz/driver-licences/converting-to-nz-driver-licence/converting-a-car-licence). INZ's Getting around page differs: "You can generally drive in New Zealand on your overseas driver licence for the first 18 months you are here. After that, you will need to convert your licence to a New Zealand driver licence." (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/getting-around-in-new-zealand/). See Uncertainties.

**Licence translation.** NZTA: "If your overseas licence isn't in English, you must also have an accurate English translation or an international driving permit (IDP). The IDP can be current or expired up to 6 months, but you must also carry your current overseas licence with you." (time-limit URL above). Translations must come from "an NZ Transport Agency authorised translation service; a diplomatic representative at a high commission, embassy or consulate; the authority that issued your overseas licence" (https://www.nzta.govt.nz/driver-licences/converting-to-nz-driver-licence/approved-translators). INZ points to the NZTA list too (INZ Getting around URL above). The conversion page says "You can't convert to a New Zealand licence using only an international driving permit."

**Certified translations for visa documents.** INZ Operational Manual A13.5: "Applicants must provide a certified translation for all documents not written in English which are provided in support of an application for a residence class visa." For a temporary entry class visa, certified translations are required for police and medical certificates not in English, and "if requested by an immigration officer" for other documents. Certified translations must "not be prepared by an applicant, any member of their family or an immigration adviser assisting with the application" and "be paid for by the applicant" (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/opsmanual/46159.htm).

**Children's records (FAMILY_WITH_CHILDREN, SOLE_PARENT).** Govt.nz: "A school may need information or documents such as: a birth certificate or passport as proof of age; medical information; legal documents; contact information." and "Make sure you enrol early to help the school plan for the year." (https://www.govt.nz/browse/education/school-and-college/how-to-enrol-your-child-at-school-in-nz/). Health NZ: "You may find it useful to have a copy of the immunisation record for you or your tamariki (children). For example, for school enrolment, B4 School Checks, boarding school or halls of residence, employment checks or when travelling overseas." This page is about NZ-held records (https://www.healthnz.govt.nz/health-topics/immunisations/get-a-copy-of-your-immunisation-record). INZ's list includes "vaccination certificates" and "birth certificates" (planning URL above).

**Home-country note.** IRD: "International tax can be complex and you may want to consult a tax professional if you're in doubt about your situation." and "If your country or territory has a double tax agreement with New Zealand, it may affect how different types of income are taxed." (https://www.ird.govt.nz/international-tax/individuals/tax-for-new-zealand-tax-residents). INZ: "You might be a tax resident in New Zealand as well as somewhere else. If both countries tax their residents' worldwide income, you could be taxed twice on the same income." (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/managing-your-money-banking-and-taxes-in-new-zealand/paying-taxes-in-new-zealand/). Neither page names the country you are leaving; see Uncertainties.

### Candidate lines

**why**
1. INZ lists documents that are "useful to have" in NZ because they make it easier to register for services. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/moving-to-new-zealand/planning-your-move-to-new-zealand/ (quote: "They will make it easier to register for services here.")

**steps**
1. Open INZ's Planning your move page and read "Bringing your documents with you". https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/moving-to-new-zealand/planning-your-move-to-new-zealand/
2. Gather the documents INZ lists: medical and vaccination records, birth and marriage certificates, qualifications, references, CV, credit references, international driver licences or permits. Same URL.
3. Try to have the original or a certified copy of each. Same URL. (quote: "Try to have the original or a certified copy of these documents.")
4. If your overseas licence is not in English, arrange an accurate English translation or an international driving permit; the permit must be accompanied by your current overseas licence. https://www.nzta.govt.nz/travelling-on-our-roads/visitors-and-new-residents/driving-on-nz-roads/time-limit-extended-for-driving-on-an-overseas-licence (figure: 6 months, "expired up to 6 months", type B)
5. Check the translation rule for any non-English documents in your visa application; INZ states it in Operational Manual A13.5. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/opsmanual/46159.htm
6. If you have children (FAMILY_WITH_CHILDREN, SOLE_PARENT): collect a birth certificate or passport as proof of age and a copy of the immunisation record. https://www.govt.nz/browse/education/school-and-college/how-to-enrol-your-child-at-school-in-nz/ and https://www.healthnz.govt.nz/health-topics/immunisations/get-a-copy-of-your-immunisation-record

**documents**
1. Medical, doctor or dental records and vaccination certificates; birth and marriage certificates; academic qualifications. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/moving-to-new-zealand/planning-your-move-to-new-zealand/
2. References from previous employers and landlords, your CV, and credit references. Same URL.
3. Your current overseas driver licence, plus an international driving permit or accurate English translation if the licence is not in English. NZTA time-limit URL above.
4. If you have children (FAMILY_WITH_CHILDREN, SOLE_PARENT): each child's birth certificate or passport, and their immunisation record. govt.nz enrolment URL and Health NZ URL above.

**mistakes**
1. Bringing an international driving permit without your physical overseas licence. NZTA: "You can't convert to a New Zealand licence using only an international driving permit." https://www.nzta.govt.nz/driver-licences/converting-to-nz-driver-licence/converting-a-car-licence
2. Leaving it late to convert: NZTA says "It may take a while to get a test booking." NZTA time-limit URL above.

**watchOuts**
1. NZTA's rule from 1 November 2026: if your last date of arrival is on or after that date, you can drive on your overseas car licence for 12 months from arrival; if before it, 18 months. (figures: 12 months and 18 months, type B; date 1 November 2026, type B; quotes in Findings.) https://www.nzta.govt.nz/travelling-on-our-roads/visitors-and-new-residents/driving-on-nz-roads/time-limit-extended-for-driving-on-an-overseas-licence
2. NZTA: "If you leave New Zealand and come back, your 12 months will start again." Same URL.
3. INZ's Getting around page still says 18 months ("for the first 18 months you are here"); NZTA's page is the one above and says it depends on your last arrival date. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/getting-around-in-new-zealand/ (figure: 18 months, type B)
4. Your overseas licence must be "current and valid" to keep driving on it. NZTA time-limit URL above.
5. INZ's translation rule depends on the visa type: residence applications need certified translations of all non-English documents; for temporary visas, police and medical certificates, and other documents if an officer asks. Certified translations cannot be prepared by you, your family or your immigration adviser. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/opsmanual/46159.htm
6. Tax across two countries: IRD says "International tax can be complex and you may want to consult a tax professional if you're in doubt about your situation." https://www.ird.govt.nz/international-tax/individuals/tax-for-new-zealand-tax-residents (proposed closest wording; the page does not name the country you are leaving)
7. INZ: "If both countries tax their residents' worldwide income, you could be taxed twice on the same income." https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/managing-your-money-banking-and-taxes-in-new-zealand/paying-taxes-in-new-zealand/
8. If you have children (FAMILY_WITH_CHILDREN, SOLE_PARENT): govt.nz says "Make sure you enrol early to help the school plan for the year." https://www.govt.nz/browse/education/school-and-college/how-to-enrol-your-child-at-school-in-nz/

**cost**
1. No cost figure is stated by an allowed page for this task. (Translation prices left out; see Deliberately not said.)

**advice**
1. INZ: "You can ask your employer or a registered immigration adviser for help, if you need it. There may be a cost for this." https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/moving-to-new-zealand/planning-your-move-to-new-zealand/
2. For visa documents, INZ says to "check what documents you will need to provide" once you decide on a visa. Same URL.
