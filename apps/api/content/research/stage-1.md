# Rebuild the roadmap around official tasks

Official NZ guidance supports **12 of the app's 21 tasks**, mostly with new names, phases or scope. It leaves **9 to retire** and adds **15 tasks the app does not have**, so the roadmap goes from 21 to 27 live tasks. The tasks that survive are the ones a government page tells a new arrival to do: SIM, bank, IRD number, tax code, GP, tenancy, driver licence, KiwiSaver, and qualification checks ([INZ When you arrive](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/when-you-arrive-in-new-zealand/)). The retired tasks fall into three groups. Home-country matters (T03, T05) go into the single generic note. Tasks no official page backs (T04 transit insurance, T15 remittances, T18 credit history, T21 joint account) become short lines in other tasks. Duplicates merge into one task (T13 into T12, T14 into T11, T20 into T10). The biggest gaps are at the border and for families. Nothing in the app covers the **NZ Traveller Declaration, which must be completed before passport control**, or what you can bring in ([Customs On your arrival](https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/travelling-to-new-zealand/on-your-arrival)). Nothing covers school enrolment either, though INZ says getting children into school "should be a priority" on arrival. Two rule changes make some current copy wrong within weeks: the overseas-licence period drops from 18 to 12 months for arrivals from **1 November 2026** (unconfirmed), and new health eligibility Regulations start on **1 December 2026** ([SL 2026/287](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/secondary-legislation/pco-drafted/2026/287/en/2026-09-21/)). Every change below stays within the approved Stage 0 limits. There are no new onboarding questions: facts onboarding does not hold get conditional copy or an opt-in task. Content stays NZ-side and informational.

Evidence legend: "unconfirmed" means the finding comes from a search snippet or from a page the fetcher could not open. It must be checked in a browser before content ships. Every other finding comes from a page opened on 25 Oct 2026.

## Nine retirements share one cause: no NZ-side official action

The pre-arrival phase has the most dead weight. INZ's planning page lists accommodation, a bank account "if you can", documents, qualification and registration paperwork, a check on prohibited goods, and choosing where to live. It says **nothing about insurance, pets or SIM cards** ([INZ Planning your move](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/moving-to-new-zealand/planning-your-move-to-new-zealand/)). T03 (overseas bank) and T05 (home-country tax) are home-country matters, so under the Stage 0 rule they go into the one generic note. Their NZ-side parts move to the tasks that own them. Opening an NZ account before arrival becomes an optional step in T07. Applying for the IRD number "online before you arrive" becomes copy in T08 ([INZ When you arrive](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/when-you-arrive-in-new-zealand/)). No official page recommends moving or transit insurance. The only official insurance advice is about health: INZ "strongly recommend[s]" comprehensive travel and health insurance, "especially [for] those who are not eligible for publicly funded health care" ([INZ Who can get public health care](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/getting-health-care-and-finding-a-doctor/who-can-get-public-health-care/)). That advice gets its own new task, T22, and T04 is retired rather than reused (see decision 1).

In the money group, T15 remittances and T21 joint account have no official source. The only related government line is Consumer Protection's warning that it is "very difficult to get your money back once it is sent overseas" (unconfirmed) ([Consumer Protection Scamwatch](https://www.consumerprotection.govt.nz/general-help/scamwatch)). That warning becomes a line in T07. T18 credit history has thin official backing: Sorted, which the Retirement Commission runs, says you can get a free credit report from each of the **three agencies** (unconfirmed) ([Sorted credit reports](https://sorted.org.nz/guides/tackling-debt/credit-reports/)). That is one sentence of content, so it becomes a note too (decision 4). T13 ACC is not an action. ACC covers accidents "regardless of residential status" and the provider lodges the claim ([Health NZ eligibility](https://www.healthnz.govt.nz/hospitals-services/eligibility-subsidies/publicly-funded-healthcare)), so it becomes an "if you're injured" note in T12. The other two merges remove duplicates. T14's rights and dispute material belongs in the signing task, T11, as an "if something goes wrong" section. T20 repeats the KiwiSaver decision already in T10, and IRD limits KiwiSaver to people "entitled to live in New Zealand indefinitely" ([IRD Joining KiwiSaver](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver/kiwisaver-individuals/joining-kiwisaver)). So the merged task applies only to the two resident visas, plus OTHER with conditional copy, as Stage 0 approved.

## Surviving tasks need new phases, dependencies and 2026 rules

Several surviving tasks are in the wrong phase or carry dependencies the official sources do not support. **T08 IRD number moves from week 1 to arrival and loses its dependency on the bank account.** IRD's new-arrival process needs a myIR account, a passport, and the INZ application number. It does not ask for an NZ bank account, and the number arrives "by text or email in 2 days" ([IRD new arrival application](https://www.ird.govt.nz/managing-my-tax/ird-numbers/ird-numbers-for-individuals/new-arrival-to-new-zealand---ird-number-application)). The deadline is the visa's final arrival date, so the copy must say so. Registering for myIR becomes step 1 of T08, not a separate task. **T07 bank's dependency on the SIM drops from hard to soft**, because INZ says you can open an account before you arrive. **T09 IR330** keeps its hard dependency on T08 and gains the penalty fact: without an IR330, tax is deducted "at the non-declaration rate of 45%" ([IRD tax codes](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-individuals/tax-codes-and-tax-rates-for-individuals/tax-codes-for-individuals)). It also gains a payslip line on the 1.75% ACC earners' levy (unconfirmed, see risks).

**T16 driver licence moves to month 1, and its 18-month copy is wrong for arrivals from 1 Nov 2026.** NZTA reportedly allows 12 months on an overseas car licence from that date, and leaving and re-entering restarts the clock (unconfirmed) ([NZTA time limit](https://www.nzta.govt.nz/travelling-on-our-roads/visitors-and-new-residents/driving-on-nz-roads/time-limit-extended-for-driving-on-an-overseas-licence)). INZ's arrival page, opened the same day, still says 18 months ([INZ When you arrive](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/when-you-arrive-in-new-zealand/)). Twelve months is not a week-1 deadline, but booking a test can take time, so month 1 is the right phase. **T17 qualification assessment moves to pre-arrival and narrows.** For SMC points from an overseas qualification you need an IQA, and its reference number goes in the EOI. Skilled migrants therefore finish it before they arrive (unconfirmed) ([INZ Check if you need an IQA](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/requirements-for-work-visas/green-list-occupations-qualifications-and-skills/check-if-you-need-an-international-qualification-assessment/)). For teachers it is required: "Before you register with the Teaching Council, you need to apply for a Teaching IQA" ([Teaching Council](https://teachingcouncil.nz/en/become-a-teacher/overseas-trained-teacher-coming-to-Aotearoa/requirements-to-teach-in-nz)). T17 should therefore show for EDUCATION and SEEKING_WORK (decision 5). **T19 CV drops LinkedIn**, which is a provider topic, and moves to pre-arrival for job seekers. Its official base is careers.govt.nz's New to New Zealand guidance (unconfirmed) ([careers.govt.nz tools](https://www.careers.govt.nz/tools/)).

**T12 GP stays in week 1 and changes its source on 1 Dec 2026.** The Healthy Futures (Pae Ora) (Eligibility) Regulations 2026 replace the 2011 Direction and "retain the eligibility settings" with targeted extensions. Under reg 8, a work visa makes you eligible if it entitles you to work for **"2 or more years"**. Under reg 13, a child under 18 of an eligible parent is covered. Under regs 19 and 20, immunisations, including measles and polio, are free regardless of eligibility ([SL 2026/287](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/secondary-legislation/pco-drafted/2026/287/en/2026-09-21/)). Because onboarding does not ask about visa length, the Stage 0 approach holds: the copy says "If your visa lets you work for 2 years or more…" and does not decide for the user. **T11 tenancy** keeps its place in week 1 and its hard dependency on the bank. Its content is confirmed: agreements must be in writing, bond is capped at **4 weeks' rent** and must be lodged within **23 working days**, letting fees and key money are banned, and the healthy homes compliance statement is required ([Tenancy Services new tenants](https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/starting-a-tenancy/new-to-tenancy/information-for-new-tenants/); [Lodging a bond](https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/rent-bond-and-bills/bond/about-lodging-a-bond/); [Letting fees](https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/rent-bond-and-bills/letting-fees-and-key-money/)). It also takes a line on setting up utilities, which INZ puts in the same sentence as finding a permanent home. Tenancy Services content may be reused only non-commercially, so this copy must be original summary with a link ([Stage 0 reuse rules, Tenancy Services Copyright](https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/about-tenancy-services/copyright/)).

## Fifteen missing tasks cluster at the border and around children

The pre-arrival gaps come straight from the Customs and INZ pages. The **NZ Traveller Declaration** "must be completed before reaching passport control". The same page sets the declaration rules: cash of **NZ$10,000 or more**, goods bought overseas over **NZ$700**, and biosecurity risk goods, with an **instant $400 fine** for failing to declare ([Customs On your arrival](https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/travelling-to-new-zealand/on-your-arrival)). Both become one task, T27 (decision 7). The household-effects concession needs a residence visa or a work visa "issued for a minimum of 12 months", and the goods must have been "personally owned and used by you before you depart" ([Customs Household effects](https://www.customs.govt.nz/travel-to-and-from-new-zealand/move-to-new-zealand-permanently/household-effects)). T25 shows these criteria as conditional copy and never tells the user whether they qualify. INZ's own document list supports T24: "Try to have the original or a certified copy of these documents", including medical, vaccination and licence records ([INZ Planning your move](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/moving-to-new-zealand/planning-your-move-to-new-zealand/)). Regulated occupations need registration before they can work, and INZ says you "must get your registration before you can apply for a visa" for these roles (unconfirmed) ([INZ occupational registration](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/requirements-for-work-visas/green-list-occupations-qualifications-and-skills/check-if-you-need-occupational-registration-for-your-job/)). T23 is therefore mostly a check that your registration and practising certificate are current before day one. Pets need the longest lead time of anything in the roadmap: an MPI vet check at least **6 months** before travel, an import permit, and at least **10 days in quarantine** for animals not coming from Australia (unconfirmed) ([MPI cats and dogs 2026](https://www.mpi.govt.nz/bring-send-to-nz/pets-travelling-to-nz/bringing-cats-and-dogs-to-nz/bringing-cats-and-dogs-to-nz-using-the-2026-import-health-standard/step-by-step-guide-to-bringing-cats-and-dogs-to-nz-2026)). Onboarding has no pet question, so T26 is opt-in (decision 2).

From arrival onward, the gaps concern work and family. INZ tells arrivals to "check your visa's work conditions" ([INZ When you arrive](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/when-you-arrive-in-new-zealand/)). An AEWV holder who changes employer needs a Job Change variation (unconfirmed) ([INZ variation of conditions](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/formshelp/application-for-a-variation-of-conditions)). Both become one pointer task, T28, which links to INZ and never interprets the conditions, as Stage 0 IAA rule 7 requires. Employment NZ backs T29. "Every employee must have a written employment agreement" ([Employment agreements](https://www.employment.govt.nz/starting-employment/employment-agreements)), and exploitation can be reported on **0800 200 088**. A substantiated report can lead to a 6-month Migrant Exploitation Protection visa ([Employment NZ migrant exploitation](https://www.employment.govt.nz/resolving-problems/types-of-problems/migrant-exploitation)). Tax residency gets its own task, T34, because the exemption has a trap. It is automatic and once-only and lasts "about 4 years", but foreign employment income "is not exempt", and claiming Working for Families ends it. Opting out "cannot" be revoked ([IRD temporary tax exemption](https://www.ird.govt.nz/roles/nz-tax-residents/exemption)). For this reason T35 Working for Families must state the trade-off and link to IRD without advising either way. Children account for four tasks: school enrolment (T30, depending on the tenancy agreement as proof of in-zone address), early childhood education and 20 Hours ECE (T31), and Well Child, immunisations and free dental care merged into one health task (T32). Most of the MoE and regional dental evidence is snippet-only. INZ's arrival page and SL 2026/287 regs 15 and 19 are opened sources. The last two are settlement support and English classes (T33), from INZ's arrival list, and the NEMA household emergency plan (T36), whose pages returned 403 (unconfirmed). Voting is left out, because enrolment needs more than a year of continuous residence (unconfirmed) ([vote.nz](https://vote.nz/local-elections/about/local-elections-2025/?lang=en-NZ)).

## Conclusion

The old roadmap was organised around what a migrant might worry about: insurance, remittances, credit, a joint account. The official sources are organised around what the state requires at each checkpoint: the border, the IRD, the employer, the landlord and the school gate. Moving to the official sequence also fixes the dependency graph. The IRD number no longer waits on a bank, and the bank no longer waits on a SIM, so the arrival phase can start in parallel instead of in a chain. The tenancy agreement becomes the hub that school, ECE, dental and the emergency plan depend on, because it is the proof of address each one needs.

The inventory is only as stable as its least-verified rows. Nine new tasks rest partly on snippets, and two dated rule changes (1 Nov and 1 Dec 2026) land before most content would ship. Run the browser checks in batch order, phase 1 first, and schedule a December re-verification. Without it, the driving and health copy will be wrong on the day it launches.
