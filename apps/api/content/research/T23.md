- HEALTHCARE: how do internationally qualified nurses register (Nursing Council, TruMerit, fee, English, hours, competence assessment)? What is said about doctors?

- TRADES: how do overseas-trained electrical workers (EWRB) and plumbers, gasfitters and drainlayers (PGDB) register and get licensed, with fees and timings?
- Which parts can be started before arrival?

## Findings

**INZ.** "In New Zealand you have to be registered with a specific organisation to work in some jobs. You must get your registration before you can apply for a visa to work in one of these roles." For example, "if you are a nurse, you must get registration from the Nursing Council of New Zealand. When you apply for a visa to work in these jobs, you will need proof that you are registered." INZ also says: "you should start the registration process as soon as possible" and "The requirements for registration can change. Contact the registration authority to find out what is needed." (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/requirements-for-work-visas/green-list-occupations-qualifications-and-skills/check-if-you-need-occupational-registration-for-your-job/). The page does not say which visa types it covers.

**Law, health practitioners (nurses, doctors and others).** HPCAA s 8(1): "Every health practitioner who practises the profession in respect of which he or she is registered must have a current practising certificate issued by the responsible authority." s 7(2) bars claiming to practise "unless the person— (a) is a health practitioner of that kind; and (b) holds a current practising certificate as a health practitioner of that kind." s 7(4) allows the statement "for the purposes of seeking employment if the person is, or would on obtaining that employment be, qualified to be registered". s 7(5): "Every person commits an offence punishable on conviction by a fine not exceeding $10,000 who contravenes this section." (https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2003/48/en/latest/#DLM203312). Doctors are covered here only through the Act.

**Nursing Council.** "You may apply to become registered as a nurse in New Zealand if you are currently registered with an overseas regulatory authority and meet Nursing Council requirements." "You must first verify your documents with TruMerit before applying to the Council to register." Documents cover identity, "current registration/s held in the last 10 years", qualifications, English, and "1,800 hours post-registration nursing experience". After verification: two free online "Welcome to Aotearoa New Zealand" courses, a criminal history check, and for "Some registered nurses and all enrolled nurses" a competence assessment. "You cannot apply directly to the Council to be registered." Timing: "The length of time it takes to process and assess IQN applications will vary." and the Council time "is separate from, and does not include, the time TruMerit takes". On employment: "You are encouraged to explore employment opportunities prior to applying for registration or coming to New Zealand." and "The Council has no role in the recruitment or employment of internationally qualified nurses." (https://www.nursingcouncil.org.nz/IQN?WebsiteKey=fa279da8-a3b1-4dad-94af-2a67fe08c81b). Fee: "The Nursing Council fee to process your application is $485 (New Zealand). There are additional costs from external providers." "All fees paid are non-refundable." The competence assessment: "Both the orientation and clinical examination must be taken in person by the nurse, at the Nurse Maude Simulation & Assessment Centre in Christchurch, New Zealand." (https://www.nursingcouncil.org.nz/IQN/Overseas_Registrations_FAQs.aspx). English: OET minimum "350 for reading, listening, and speaking; and ... 300 for writing", or IELTS Academic "7 for reading, listening, and speaking; and ... 6.5 for writing"; "We do not accept PTE." Hours: "It is important that you apply to TruMerit after you have achieved the required 1,800 practice hours." (https://www.nursingcouncil.org.nz/IQN/H5.aspx). Nurses with a current Australian practising certificate use a separate TTMR process (IQN page).

**Teaching Council.** "every teacher in Aotearoa must be registered and hold a current practising certificate to teach." "You will not be able to teach with registration alone. A practising certificate is what allows you to teach legally in New Zealand." The application needs an Educator Sector Login and My Rawa account, qualifications and transcripts, "Teaching IQA report (if applicable)", English competency, "overseas Police clearance", professional standing statements, proof of identity form, testimonials, assessment of teaching practice, CV and certified copies. "Applications usually take 6-12 weeks to process, depending on complexity, and may take longer if a panel review is required." (https://teachingcouncil.nz/en/become-a-teacher/overseas-trained-teacher-coming-to-Aotearoa/register-to-teach-as-an-overseas-teacher). Teaching IQA: "Before you register with the Teaching Council, you need to apply for a Teaching IQA to register as a teacher in New Zealand." Police vets are needed for "any country you have lived in for 12 months or more in the 10 years before you made your application", or a statutory declaration if unable to obtain one (https://teachingcouncil.nz/en/become-a-teacher/overseas-trained-teacher-coming-to-Aotearoa/requirements-to-teach-in-nz). Overseas police checks: "For most countries, you'll apply through AIS International ... Applicants pay AIS International directly"; "These arrangements are currently being trialled until 4 December 2026." (https://teachingcouncil.nz/en/become-a-teacher/third-party-checks/overseas-police-clearance). Language: "you need to show you're confident using English or te reo Māori" (https://teachingcouncil.nz/en/become-a-teacher/overseas-trained-teacher-coming-to-Aotearoa/language-competency-requirements).

**EWRB.** "All electrical workers must hold a Practising Licence to carry out prescribed electrical work. If you are an overseas-trained applicant, you must hold a Limited Certificate before you can apply for a Practising Licence." Apply in the EW Portal. After an assessment letter and invitation, "you will need to work under supervision in New Zealand"; the letter lists hours, "short courses, examinations, and practical assessments". "The registration application fee as an overseas-trained applicant is $1650 (including GST)." "your application should be processed within 12 weeks." (https://www.ewrb.govt.nz/licences/becoming-registered-for-overseas-trained-applicants/). Electrician class: "Completed 4 years (8000 hours) practical training and experience as an Electrician overseas, including: At least 1 year (2000 hours) of experience of wiring installation within buildings". References must be certified, on letterhead, signed and dated; "falsifying work references is fraud"; "Do not apply if you have not completed the required hours or cannot provide evidence of employment, as your application will be declined and the application fee will not be refunded." (https://www.ewrb.govt.nz/registration/registration-overseas-trained-pathway/electrician/). 8 October 2026 notice: "Electrical workers must also be competent to undertake the work they perform, and hold registration and a current practising licence." (https://www.ewrb.govt.nz/about-us/news-and-notices/getting-prescribed-electrical-work-right/).

**PGDB.** "In New Zealand sanitary plumbing, gasfitting and drainlaying work is restricted and can only be carried out by practitioners who are registered and licensed to do so." (https://www.pgdb.co.nz/apply_for_registration/). "If you are moving to New Zealand and want to carry out plumbing, gasfitting and/or drainlaying, you will need to register and apply for a licence. Carrying out restricted plumbing, gasfitting and drainlaying work without a current New Zealand licence is illegal." The page lists a formal overseas qualification "equivalent to or greater than the New Zealand Certificate (level 4)" and "a minimum of four years plumbing or gasfitting, or two years drainlaying full-time work experience" as the basis for a possible pathway. If the Overseas Qualification Pathway Application succeeds, "you can apply for a provisional licence which will allow you to carry out work for up to 12 months while you prepare to sit the registration exam." Submit by emailing RFR@pgdb.co.nz (https://www.pgdb.co.nz/apply_for_registration/register_with_overseas_qualifications/). Fees "effective from 1 April 2026" (incl. GST): registration NZ$350, annual practising licence NZ$100, disciplinary and prosecution levy NZ$275, first-time total NZ$725 if no other licence held, provisional licence NZ$100 per trade, exam NZ$330 per trade, Certifying exam NZ$410 per trade (https://www.pgdb.co.nz/apply_for_registration/fees/).

**steps**
1. Read INZ's page on occupational registration to see whether your job is one that needs it, then contact the registration authority. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/requirements-for-work-visas/green-list-occupations-qualifications-and-skills/check-if-you-need-occupational-registration-for-your-job/
2. Start the registration process as soon as possible, as INZ advises. Same URL.
3. [HEALTHCARE, nurses] Verify your documents with TruMerit first. The Council says you cannot apply to it directly. https://www.nursingcouncil.org.nz/IQN?WebsiteKey=fa279da8-a3b1-4dad-94af-2a67fe08c81b
4. [HEALTHCARE, nurses] After verification, complete the two online Welcome to Aotearoa courses and the criminal history check, and, where the Council requires it, the competence assessment. Same URL.
5. [HEALTHCARE, doctors and other health professions] Check with the authority responsible for your profession. The Act requires a current practising certificate from "the responsible authority". https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2003/48/en/latest/#DLM203312
6. [EDUCATION] Get your Teaching IQA first where it applies, then apply through My Rawa using "Maintain my practising certificate". https://teachingcouncil.nz/en/become-a-teacher/overseas-trained-teacher-coming-to-Aotearoa/requirements-to-teach-in-nz and https://teachingcouncil.nz/en/become-a-teacher/overseas-trained-teacher-coming-to-Aotearoa/register-to-teach-as-an-overseas-teacher
7. [EDUCATION] Order overseas police clearances for countries where you lived 12 months or more in the last 10 years. https://teachingcouncil.nz/en/become-a-teacher/overseas-trained-teacher-coming-to-Aotearoa/requirements-to-teach-in-nz · Fact: 12 months in 10 years; "any country you have lived in for 12 months or more in the 10 years before you made your application"; same URL; B.
8. [TRADES, electrical] Apply in the EW Portal. Overseas-trained applicants must hold a Limited Certificate before applying for a Practising Licence, and supervised work in New Zealand follows. https://www.ewrb.govt.nz/licences/becoming-registered-for-overseas-trained-applicants/
9. [TRADES, plumbing, gasfitting, drainlaying] Submit an Overseas Qualification Pathway Application with evidence, then apply for a provisional licence if the Board outcome allows. https://www.pgdb.co.nz/apply_for_registration/register_with_overseas_qualifications/
