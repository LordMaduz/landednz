## Findings

**Who decides, and where to check.** INZ: "The Ministry of Health decides who is eligible for publicly funded health care. If you qualify, you do not have to pay for these services in New Zealand." It tells you to check Health NZ to find out "if you can get publicly funded health care; if you are eligible for full health care or only limited services; what you need to prove you are eligible" (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/getting-health-care-and-finding-a-doctor/who-can-get-public-health-care/). Health NZ's guide: "Check the options below to see what the eligibility requirements are for your situation. You will also find out what proof you will need to give to your healthcare provider." It has sections "Changes in December 2026 / Check if you are eligible / Limited range of publicly funded health services / If you are not eligible" and shows "Last updated 15 October 2026" (https://www.tewhatuora.govt.nz/corporate-information/our-health-system/eligibility-for-publicly-funded-health-services/guide-to-eligibility-for-public-health-services). No separate checker tool was found. The Health NZ eligibility page has a "Check your eligibility" button (https://www.tewhatuora.govt.nz/hospitals-services/eligibility-subsidies/publicly-funded-healthcare).

**Which rules apply when.** Health NZ: "The eligibility criteria is set out in the Health and Disability Services Eligibility Direction 2011" and "Changes to who can get publicly funded health services in New Zealand will take effect from 1 December 2026" (Health NZ guide URL above). The Healthy Futures (Pae Ora) (Eligibility) Regulations 2026, SL 2026/287, were made 21 September 2026, show "Not yet in force", and say "These regulations come into force on 1 December 2026" (reg 2). Their explanatory note: "These regulations retain the eligibility settings specified in the Direction with the following key changes", including extension to holders of a Migrant Exploitation Protection Work Visa or a Victims of Family Violence Work Visa, and extension of measles and polio immunisations to people otherwise ineligible (https://www.legislation.govt.nz/secondary-legislation/pco-drafted/2026/287/en/2026-09-21/). So the Direction applies up to 30 November 2026 and the regulations from 1 December 2026. Reg 4: a person is eligible for a service if "at the time they receive those services" they are in New Zealand and meet the criteria for it. Reg 5: "The Ministry must, if requested in writing to do so, determine any question or dispute concerning whether a person is eligible to receive services funded under the Act."

**Work visas (duration test).** Health NZ (Direction): "a work visa that either lets you stay in Aotearoa New Zealand: for 2 years or more (your work visa starts on your first day in Aotearoa); for a length of time equal to, or more than, 2 years (including the time you have legally already been here before getting the visa)". Proof: "your valid passport endorsed (stamped) with a current work visa (or work permit)". Reg 8 (from 1 December 2026): "a work visa that entitles the person to work in New Zealand for— (i) 2 or more years; or (ii) together with the period of time the person has already been lawfully in New Zealand immediately before obtaining the work visa, 2 or more years". Residents: Health NZ lists "a residence class visa; a resident visa; a permanent resident visa"; reg 6 lists "holds a residence class visa". INZ, for temporary visas: "In general, people who have temporary visas to visit, study and work in New Zealand cannot get publicly funded health care in New Zealand." Note: "Check the conditions of the visa you are applying for to see if you are eligible." Also: "Most people who have resident visas and live in New Zealand can get publicly funded health care."

**Children and partners.** Health NZ (Direction): children "17 years old or younger" in the care and control of a parent, legal guardian or a person adopting or becoming guardian, who must themselves be in an eligible group (the list includes a work visa holder). Proof is the parent's or guardian's proof plus "your passport and proof of relationship" or a birth certificate. Reg 3: "child means a person under the age of 18 years". Reg 13(1): a child is eligible if in the care and control of "a parent or guardian of the child who is eligible under any of regulations 6 to 11". Health NZ on partners: "Except for maternity services, partners of people eligible for publicly funded health and disability services must themselves meet the eligibility criteria." The regulations have no general partner-of-work-visa-holder class in the notes; the only partner clause is reg 11(b) for scholarship recipients. INZ: "Most practices offer free doctor visits for children under 14 years." Reg 15 covers child health and development checks such as Well Child Tamariki Ora; regs 19 and 20 cover immunisations ("A child is eligible to receive immunisations ... listed on the National Immunisation Schedule"; "A person is eligible to receive immunisations ... for measles and poliomyelitis"). Source for regs: legislation URL above.

**Pregnancy.** INZ: "If you are pregnant and apply for a temporary visa to visit, study, or work in New Zealand, you must tell us how you will pay for your maternity care while you are here." Also "Depending on the visa you are applying for and your nationality you may be eligible for publicly funded health care during and after your pregnancy. If you are not eligible you must cover the costs of your own maternity care", and "you must show you have a minimum of NZD $9,000 to pay for your maternity care". Its list of eligible situations includes "have a partner who is eligible for publicly funded health care", and: "Note If you have a Limited Visa, you cannot get publicly funded maternity care." (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/process-to-apply/once-you-have-a-visa/bringing-family-to-new-zealand/if-you-are-pregnant/). INZ's health page: "If you are pregnant, you must be able to cover the costs of your maternity care if you: have a temporary visa to visit, study or work in New Zealand; cannot get publicly funded health care, and; will be here during your pregnancy or for the birth." Health NZ (Direction): "If you are pregnant, you are eligible if you or your husband, civil union partner or de facto partner is one of the following: ..." with groups that include a work visa holder for 2 years or more. "Most maternity care is free for eligible pregnant women"; there "may be charges for some services such as: antenatal or childbirth education classes; some tests at a private laboratory; private obstetricians; private maternity hospitals". Reg 16(1) (from 1 December 2026): "A pregnant woman is eligible to receive the services specified in subclause (2) if the pregnant woman's unborn child will be eligible under regulation 13 to receive services funded under the Act when it is born."

**Proof, enrolment, insurance.** INZ: "you will need to show your health care provider proof of this when you visit, for example your e-Visa letter. If you do not have any proof, you may have to pay." Enrolment: "You can only enrol if you are eligible for publicly funded health care services." and "You will pay reduced doctors' fees if you enrol with a practice." If not covered: "you, or your sponsor if you have one, must pay for any health care or medical assistance you may need in New Zealand. We strongly recommend that people travelling to New Zealand, especially those who are not eligible for publicly funded health care, have comprehensive travel insurance, including full health insurance." Also: "If you want to take out health insurance in New Zealand, you may need to provide proof that you are eligible for subsidised public health care." Health NZ: "The New Zealand Government strongly recommends that people in Aotearoa who are not eligible for publicly funded health services hold comprehensive travel insurance, including health insurance." INZ: "You must provide evidence that you have health insurance when applying for a Fee-paying Student Visa and some working holiday visas." Waits: "Sometimes you may have to wait to be treated under the public health system because of high demand." Dental: "You must pay privately for most dental services." (INZ health URL above.)

**ACC (INZ's wording only).** INZ: "If you are injured in an accident while you are in New Zealand, the Accident Compensation Scheme (ACC) will usually cover most of the costs of treatment and rehabilitation for residents as well as visitors on a temporary visa." "Your doctor or health provider will make an ACC claim for you. ACC must accept the claim before it will contribute to funding." (INZ health URL above.) The ACC page was not opened.

### Candidate lines

**why**
- Eligible people do not pay for publicly funded services; the Ministry of Health decides who is eligible. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/getting-health-care-and-finding-a-doctor/who-can-get-public-health-care/ ("If you qualify, you do not have to pay for these services in New Zealand.")
- If you are not covered, you or your sponsor pay for health care, and health insurance may ask for proof of eligibility. Same URL ("you, or your sponsor if you have one, must pay for any health care or medical assistance you may need in New Zealand.")

**steps**
- Read Health NZ's guide to eligibility and use its "Check if you are eligible" section to find the group that matches your visa. https://www.tewhatuora.govt.nz/corporate-information/our-health-system/eligibility-for-publicly-funded-health-services/guide-to-eligibility-for-public-health-services ("Check the options below to see what the eligibility requirements are for your situation.")
- Note which rules apply on the date you will receive care: the 2011 Direction until 30 November 2026, and SL 2026/287 from 1 December 2026. https://www.legislation.govt.nz/secondary-legislation/pco-drafted/2026/287/en/2026-09-21/ — fact: 1 December 2026, "These regulations come into force on 1 December 2026.", same URL, B
- Read the criteria for your own visa. Health NZ and reg 8 set a work-visa duration test: 2 years or more, which can include time already lawfully in New Zealand. https://www.tewhatuora.govt.nz/corporate-information/our-health-system/eligibility-for-publicly-funded-health-services/guide-to-eligibility-for-public-health-services — fact: 2 years, "for 2 years or more (your work visa starts on your first day in Aotearoa)", Health NZ guide URL, B
- Look up each family member separately. Health NZ says partners "must themselves meet the eligibility criteria" except for maternity services, and has a separate child section. Same Health NZ URL.
- If anyone is pregnant, read INZ's pregnancy page and the Health NZ pregnancy criteria, which refer to the partner's or unborn child's status. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/process-to-apply/once-you-have-a-visa/bringing-family-to-new-zealand/if-you-are-pregnant/ ("Depending on the visa you are applying for and your nationality you may be eligible ...")
- Check what proof each group is asked to show a provider, and keep it with you. Same Health NZ URL ("You will also find out what proof you will need to give to your healthcare provider.")
- If a question or dispute remains, the regulations let you ask the Ministry in writing for a determination (from 1 December 2026). https://www.legislation.govt.nz/secondary-legislation/pco-drafted/2026/287/en/2026-09-21/ ("The Ministry must, if requested in writing to do so, determine any question or dispute ...")
- If Health NZ's criteria show cover does not apply to someone, look at comprehensive travel or health insurance. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/getting-health-care-and-finding-a-doctor/who-can-get-public-health-care/ ("We strongly recommend ... comprehensive travel insurance, including full health insurance.")

**documents**
- Health NZ names your valid passport endorsed with a current work visa (or work permit) as proof for work-visa holders. Health NZ guide URL ("your valid passport endorsed (stamped) with a current work visa (or work permit)")
- INZ gives your e-Visa letter as an example of proof to show a provider. INZ health URL ("for example your e-Visa letter")
- For a child, Health NZ lists a parent's or guardian's proof plus your passport and proof of relationship, or a birth certificate. Health NZ guide URL ("your passport and proof of relationship")

**mistakes**
- Assuming a partner is covered because you are. Health NZ guide URL ("partners of people eligible ... must themselves meet the eligibility criteria", except maternity).
- Turning up without proof. INZ health URL ("If you do not have any proof, you may have to pay.")
- Assuming one page covers every date. Health NZ's guide still cites the 2011 Direction and announces the change from 1 December 2026. Health NZ guide URL.

**watchOuts**
- INZ says temporary visa holders can in general not get publicly funded care and to check the conditions of your visa; Health NZ and the regulations list the work-visa test. INZ health URL, Health NZ guide URL.
- INZ says that if you are pregnant on a temporary visa and cannot get public care, you must be able to cover maternity costs, and that you must show a minimum of NZD $9,000 in some cases. INZ pregnant page — fact: NZD $9,000, "you must show you have a minimum of NZD $9,000 to pay for your maternity care", INZ pregnant URL, A
- INZ says holders of a Limited Visa cannot get publicly funded maternity care. INZ pregnant URL ("If you have a Limited Visa, you cannot get publicly funded maternity care.")
- INZ says ACC will usually cover most treatment costs after an accident for residents and temporary-visa visitors, and ACC must accept the claim first. INZ health URL ("ACC must accept the claim before it will contribute to funding.")

**cost**
- Enrolled patients pay reduced doctors' fees, and INZ says most practices offer free visits for children under 14. INZ health URL — fact: 14 years, "Most practices offer free doctor visits for children under 14 years.", INZ health URL, B
- Health NZ says some maternity-related items may carry charges, such as antenatal classes, private laboratory tests, private obstetricians and private maternity hospitals. Health NZ guide URL ("There may be charges for some services such as: ...")

**advice**
- For visa questions use INZ or a licensed immigration adviser; INZ's page only points you to Health NZ for eligibility. INZ health URL ("Check the Health New Zealand Te Whatu Ora website")
- For a disputed eligibility question, the Ministry decides (written request, from 1 December 2026). https://www.legislation.govt.nz/secondary-legislation/pco-drafted/2026/287/en/2026-09-21/ (reg 5)
