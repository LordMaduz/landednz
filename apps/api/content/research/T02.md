## Questions

- What does INZ say about arranging first accommodation and who needs it?
- Which short-stay options does INZ list, and what does it say about cost and availability?
- Does tenancy law cover short stays (hotels, motels, holiday homes, boarding houses, short fixed-term tenancies)?
- Which money rules (bond, rent in advance, letting fees, option fees) apply if a short stay is a tenancy?
- What do official pages say about checking a place, signing, and scams before paying?

## Uncertainties

- **Boarding houses and short stays.** INZ says a boarding house tenant has "their own agreement with the landlord". The Tenancy Services "does not protect" page lists "boarders" as not covered. The Tenancy Services boarding-houses page says "Boarding house tenancies and standard tenancies share many of the same requirements." The notes suggest "boarders" there means someone boarding with the owner, but that was not verified. What would settle it: read the Tenancy Services boarding-houses pages ("Are you operating a boarding house?") or a Tenancy Services statement on the term.
- **Rental-scam wording.** No allowed page found that says not to pay a deposit before seeing a place. Closest: INZ "Always visit a property before you commit to renting it" and the generic Consumer Protection steps. What would settle it: a dedicated rental-scam page on consumerprotection.govt.nz or ncsc.govt.nz, found by site search in a browser.
- **Hotel and motel cancellation or refund rules.** No allowed source found, and hotels and motels sit outside the Act per Tenancy Services. What would settle it: an official consumer page on accommodation bookings.
- **Whether a given short stay is a tenancy.** Tenancy Services says "If a residential tenancy is not one of the types mentioned above, then it is most likely covered by the Act." Which side a particular arrangement falls on is not something a page decides. What would settle it: Tenancy Services' own answer for the arrangement.
- **Work and Income help.** The notes record the Tenancy Services line that Work and Income "can help with bond and accommodation support in advance" and that "You must meet the criteria to be eligible". Eligibility for migrants was not checked. What would settle it: a Work and Income page on accommodation support and visa holders.
- **Short fixed-term quote.** The 90-day quote comes from an example on a page written for landlords. What would settle it: re-read the page and the Act's wording before publishing.

## Findings

**Plan first accommodation.** INZ: "Think about where you want to live, or where you might need to live for work. It is a good idea to organise accommodation for when you first arrive. You could stay with family or friends, in a hotel." (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/moving-to-new-zealand/planning-your-move-to-new-zealand/). The temporary-accommodation page adds: "Unless you will be staying with family or have accommodation organised by an employer or education provider, you will need somewhere to live for the first few weeks while you look for something more permanent." (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/finding-somewhere-to-live/hotels-motels-and-other-places-to-live-temporarily/).

**Options and cost.** Same INZ page: "Many new arrivals will start their New Zealand experience in a hotel or motel, holiday rental, or a cabin at a holiday park or camping ground." On cost: "The cost of short-term accommodation, and how easy it is to find, will vary throughout the year. It can be affected by tourist numbers, university start dates and school holidays, among other things." Booking: "There are lots of booking websites you can use to find somewhere to stay... they may list self-catering apartments, backpackers and guest houses (also known as bed-and-breakfasts or 'B and Bs')." and "If you book a hotel or motel, always remember to ask for a quote for a longer stay." Holiday homes: "In the North Island, a holiday home may be called a bach (pronounced 'batch'), or in the South Island, a crib." Parks: "many holiday parks and camping grounds have cabins, 'tourist flats', motel options and self-contained units. These can be a cheaper solution for people needing short-term accommodation. Be aware that sometimes holiday parks and camping grounds have shared facilities, such as a kitchen, bathroom and laundry." Boarding houses: "For short-term accommodation, you can stay at a boarding house. These are sometimes advertised as shared accommodation or 'rooms to rent'. At a boarding house, each tenant has their own agreement with the landlord to rent a single room, or a sleeping area in a room they share with other tenants. They also share facilities, for example the kitchen and bathroom." (all on the INZ temporary-accommodation URL above).

**Does tenancy law cover a short stay?** Tenancy Services: "Residential Tenancies Act does not cover flatmates; boarders; ... student accommodation (exceptions apply – see Section 5B of the Act); holiday homes; hotels and motels; hospitals and rest-homes; commercial tenancies; transitional and emergency housing..." and "The Act does not apply when the house is being rented for the tenant’s holiday purposes. In other words, the tenant actually lives somewhere else and is just staying in the house for a holiday. Typically, holiday houses are let for short periods." Also: "If a residential tenancy is not one of the types mentioned above, then it is most likely covered by the Act." (https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/starting-a-tenancy/who-is-protected/who-tenancy-law-does-not-protect/). On the boarding-house question, see Uncertainties: the INZ page and the Tenancy Services boarding-houses page (https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/starting-a-tenancy/types-of-tenancies/boarding-houses/, "Boarding house tenancies and standard tenancies share many of the same requirements.") are cited as written, without a ruling here. Short fixed-term: "owners could rent out the property for 90 days (or less) and the tenancy will end on the expiry date." (https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/starting-a-tenancy/types-of-tenancies/short-fixed-term-tenancy/; page is for landlords, quote is from an example).

**Money rules where a short stay is a tenancy.** Letting fees: "Landlords or their agents can’t charge letting fees to tenants." Key money: "Landlords are not allowed to charge key money. The only exception is an ‘option fee’... The maximum amount for an option fee is one week’s rent." Also: "Prospective tenants are encouraged to talk with the landlord and agree in writing how long they have to decide... and what happens with the money paid as option fee if they decide not to rent it." (https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/rent-bond-and-bills/letting-fees-and-key-money/, last updated 15 March 2021). Rent in advance: "A landlord can ask for 1 or 2 weeks’ rent in advance... It is unlawful for a landlord to ask the tenant to pay more than 2 weeks’ rent in advance." (https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/rent-bond-and-bills/rent/charging-rent/). Bond and moving in: "The bond can be up to 4 weeks’ rent, a receipt must be provided to the tenants. The bond must be lodged digitally within 23 working days." Also "Inspect the property with your landlord and record the condition and any damage." and "Only sign a tenancy agreement when you are sure you want the property." (https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/starting-a-tenancy/new-to-tenancy/information-for-new-tenants/). Where the Act does not apply: "If the tenancy arrangement is not covered by the Residential Tenancies Act 1986 (the Act), a bond should not be lodged with Tenancy Services for that tenancy." (https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/rent-bond-and-bills/bond/preventing-problems-with-bonds/, last updated 29 June 2026).

**Checking a place and scams.** INZ: "Always visit a property before you commit to renting it." and "There can be high demand for good rental properties, so it is a good idea to make contact quickly." and "You must pay your rent on time, even if there is a problem with the property or your landlord." (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/finding-somewhere-to-live/renting-a-place-to-live-in-new-zealand/). Tenancy Services: "Search for Tenancy Tribunal orders on the Ministry of Justice website (external link) to find out if the landlord has been involved in disputes in the past." (information-for-new-tenants URL above). Consumer Protection (a news item, not rental-specific): "Stop and think before clicking, sharing, or making any payments. Check the source to make sure it’s real. Report scams when you see them." and "If you have been targeted by a scam: stop all contact with the scammer; do not make any more payments; contact the bank or service you sent money through." (https://www.consumerprotection.govt.nz/news-and-media/scammers-defraud-kiwis-of-265-million).

**Support.** Tenancy Services new-tenants page: "there may be financial support available. Work and Income New Zealand can help with bond and accommodation support in advance. You must meet the criteria to be eligible." (information-for-new-tenants URL above).

**steps**
- Start with INZ's page on hotels, motels and other places to live temporarily. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/finding-somewhere-to-live/hotels-motels-and-other-places-to-live-temporarily/
- Compare the options INZ lists: hotel or motel, holiday rental, or a cabin at a holiday park or camping ground. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/finding-somewhere-to-live/hotels-motels-and-other-places-to-live-temporarily/
- Use a booking website to find a place, and for a hotel or motel ask for a quote for a longer stay. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/finding-somewhere-to-live/hotels-motels-and-other-places-to-live-temporarily/
- Check the facilities at holiday parks, since INZ says some have shared kitchen, bathroom and laundry. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/finding-somewhere-to-live/hotels-motels-and-other-places-to-live-temporarily/
- If the stay is a rental, visit the property before you commit. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/finding-somewhere-to-live/renting-a-place-to-live-in-new-zealand/
- Tenancy Services suggests searching Tenancy Tribunal orders on the Ministry of Justice website to see if a landlord has had past disputes. https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/starting-a-tenancy/new-to-tenancy/information-for-new-tenants/
- Sign a tenancy agreement only when you are sure you want the property, and record its condition with the landlord. https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/starting-a-tenancy/new-to-tenancy/information-for-new-tenants/

**documents**
- Tenancy Services says a bond receipt must be provided to tenants; keep it. https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/starting-a-tenancy/new-to-tenancy/information-for-new-tenants/
- Tenancy Services encourages agreeing in writing how long you have to decide, and what happens to any option fee if you do not rent. https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/rent-bond-and-bills/letting-fees-and-key-money/

**mistakes**
- Booking a hotel or motel without asking for a quote for a longer stay. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/finding-somewhere-to-live/hotels-motels-and-other-places-to-live-temporarily/

- Making payments before checking the source is real; Consumer Protection says to stop and think first. https://www.consumerprotection.govt.nz/news-and-media/scammers-defraud-kiwis-of-265-million

**watchOuts**
- Tenancy Services says the Residential Tenancies Act does not cover hotels and motels or holiday homes rented for a holiday. https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/starting-a-tenancy/who-is-protected/who-tenancy-law-does-not-protect/
- Tenancy Services says a short fixed-term tenancy can run 90 days or less and ends on its expiry date. https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/starting-a-tenancy/types-of-tenancies/short-fixed-term-tenancy/
- INZ describes boarding-house tenants as each having their own agreement with the landlord; Tenancy Services lists "boarders" among those the Act does not cover and has a separate boarding-houses section.
