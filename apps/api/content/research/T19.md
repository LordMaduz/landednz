## Uncertainties

- **Is there an official rule on leaving a photo, date of birth, age or marital status off a CV?** No allowed page opened says so. Tahatū says "don't use images" and its workbook checklist says "Images, photos, text boxes and tables have been removed." Its CV contents list does not include age or marital status, but it never says to leave them out. What would settle it: an official Tahatū or Human Rights Commission page stating it, or the old careers "tips-for-creating-a-nz-style-cv" page if it still loads.
- **CV length: 2 pages or 2 to 3?** The Tahatū CV guide says "keep your CV to 2 pages if you can"; the Tahatū workbooks say "no more than two to three pages". Both are on tahatu.govt.nz. What would settle it: Tahatū stating which applies.
- **Current pay and skill-level conditions for a partner's visa to be supported.** Thresholds on INZ pages were only partly read, and search results hinted at differing figures. What would settle it: reading the full "Your supporting partner's visa" section of the INZ Partner of a Worker Work Visa page.
- **Does the retired CV Builder still exist anywhere?** The govt.nz job-hunting page still links to it; the retirement itself came only from a search summary. What would settle it: Tahatū or govt.nz stating the status.
- **Does Employment NZ say the agreement must be in hand before starting?** The pages opened say the employee "should read the employment agreement" before agreeing to take a job; no page opened says "before you start" in so many words. A 90-day trial claim is unconfirmed (search result only). What would settle it: an Employment NZ page stating the timing.
- **Work and Income CV help for migrants.** The "CVs and cover letters" page linked from govt.nz was not opened. What would settle it: opening it.

## Findings

**Where to start.** careers.govt.nz now redirects to Tahatū (HTTP 301 from https://www.careers.govt.nz/tools/ to https://tahatu.govt.nz). INZ points job seekers there: "You can find tips on how to find and apply for a job at Tahatū Career Navigator." (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/finding-work-in-new-zealand/how-to-find-a-job-in-new-zealand/). The CV guide, updated 29 Sep 2026, offers "tips, examples and templates" (https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv/). Tahatū has templates and examples, not its own CV builder.

**Tailoring and type.** "Update your CV for each job application using information and keywords from the job ad." Two types: "Skills-focused CV – useful for school leavers, people with no work experience, and people with gaps in work history." and "Work-focused CV – useful for people with work experience, people changing jobs, and people moving to the next career stage." (same Tahatū CV URL).

**Format and length.** "use a plain black font, like Calibri 11 ... use bullet points ... use short sentences and short paragraphs ... don't use images ... keep your CV to 2 pages if you can." (same URL). The Tahatū workbook checklist reads "CV is no more than two to three pages." and "Images, photos, text boxes and tables have been removed." (https://tahatu.govt.nz/api/documents/serve/233/Applying_for_jobs_CVs_cover_letters_and_more_workbook.pdf). The two pages differ on length; see Uncertainties.

**Contents.** Name and contact details at the top ("a professional email address that ideally includes your name"), a personal statement, key skills and experience, work history with employer, job title, location and dates (most recent first), education and qualifications, optional interests. "You don't need to list all your work history – it's OK to keep your CV short." (same Tahatū CV URL). The guide does not list age or marital status; it does not say to omit them either.

**Keywords and file.** "your CV may go through an applicant tracking system (ATS)... It's important to write your CV using key words and sentences from the job advertisement... Keep the formatting simple and consistent." Send "in a PDF format unless the employer asks for a Word document" and name the file with your first and last name and CV, "for example, sam-henderson-CV.pdf." (same URL). The checklist adds: "have a Word version ready in case an employer asks for that format" and "check that your application was sent and received" (https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/job-application-checklist/).

**Gaps and referees.** "Explain the reason for big gaps in your cover letter." "Include referees in your CV if the job ad asks for them. You'll need contact details for at least 2... Otherwise, write 'Referees available on request' in this section and use your referees if you get an interview." (Tahatū CV URL). The workbook says to "let them know first" (https://tahatu.govt.nz/api/documents/serve/159/Job_Hunters_Workbook__Web_version.pdf).

**Cover letter.** "Employers will usually read your cover letter before they look at your CV"; "keep your cover letter to 1 page"; "don't use images"; address it to the contact person in the ad; include "how your skills and experience match the job" (https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cover-letter/, updated 02 Dec 2025).

**Third-party CV builders.** Tahatū: "If you use a CV builder, you can check Tahatū CV templates and examples to make sure your CV is in a similar New Zealand style." It tells users to check fees and hidden costs, and for privacy to check where data is stored ("New Zealand or Australia preferred"); "You shouldn't share sensitive personal data, such as bank account numbers or passwords." Builders it lists are "run by other organisations and aren't linked to Tahatū" (Tahatū CV URL).

**Searching for jobs.** INZ: "Most people in New Zealand look for jobs on employment websites or by contacting recruitment companies. They also contact employers directly." Also "Many of them post jobs from employers expecting applications from people already in New Zealand. Some specialise in connecting with people overseas." and "approaching employers directly is an accepted and often successful approach." Sector sites it lists include Kiwi Health Jobs, Education Gazette and Engineering New Zealand. "You do not need to pay the company or agency as they get paid by the employer when they place someone in a job." Registration and qualifications: "Depending on your job, you may be required to register with the relevant professional body to get a job and a visa." (INZ how-to-find-a-job URL above).

**Offers and agreements.** INZ: "You can legally offer a job to an overseas candidate before they have a work or resident visa." The offer should include a clause "Subject to immigration criteria being met". Employers must check right to work: "Before someone from overseas can begin working for you, you must check that their visa gives them the right to work in New Zealand." (INZ rules-for-offering URL and check-if-someone-can-legally-work URL above). Employment NZ: "Every employee must have a written employment agreement." and "Before agreeing to take on a job, the employee should read the employment agreement, ask questions about anything they do not understand and get professional advice if they want to." (https://www.employment.govt.nz/starting-employment/employment-agreements/creating-an-employment-agreement). "Migrant workers have the same minimum employment rights as New Zealand workers." (https://www.employment.govt.nz/resolving-problems/migrant-exploitation).

**Questions at hiring.** Employment NZ lists grounds including "age", "marital or family status" and "ethnicity or national origins", and says "it is unlawful to ask questions of or about a job applicant that indicate an intention to discriminate on one of the grounds covered by the Act." (https://www.employment.govt.nz/resolving-problems/bullying-harassment-and-discrimination/discrimination). This page is about what employers ask, not about what a CV should contain.

**If your partner will look for work.** INZ: "This visa allows you to work in New Zealand while your partner is here on a work visa. Your partner must have an eligible work visa to support your application." "You do not need to have a New Zealand job offer to apply." Cost "From NZD $1630". "You can do any work, in any job, anywhere in New Zealand, as long as: the work you do is legal ... you have any occupational registration that is needed" (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/partner-of-a-worker-work-visa/). A partner's visa conditions: "Your visa conditions are recorded in your eVisa." and "You must only work under the conditions specified in your visa." (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/requirements-for-work-visas/check-or-change-your-work-visa-conditions/). Whether a partner's visa can be supported depends on the supporting partner's visa; for an AEWV holder, INZ says it "depends on their job's skill level and how much they earn" (Partner of a Worker Work Visa URL). INZ and Employment NZ do not give a verdict on any individual case; Employment NZ "does not provide immigration advice".

**Job scams.** INZ: "Watch out if you are asked to pay fees for your job, sometimes called 'processing' or 'placement' fees. New Zealand employers cannot charge you fees for a job or make you pay any of their recruitment costs." "Immigration New Zealand only charges you an application fee." "We will never ask you to pay for visa application fees by depositing money into a bank account." Be wary of "'package' offers with a visa, job and accommodation." "Official email addresses from Immigration New Zealand end with 'immigration.govt.nz' or 'mbie.govt.nz'." (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/protecting-yourself-from-immigration-scams/). Also "An employer or a recruiter cannot charge you for a job." (INZ news-centre URL above). Employment NZ lists having to "pay an amount of money or a fee to get a job" as a sign of exploitation (migrant-exploitation URL above). Own Your Online red flags include "The job listing is only on a single board", "Communication moves quickly to an instant messenger service" and "The employer contacts you out of the blue or offers an interview or a job straight away"; "A genuine employer won't need your bank account details until you have accepted a job." (https://www.ownyouronline.govt.nz/personal/get-protected/guides/protect-yourself-from-job-scams/). Consumer Protection describes advance fee fraud, including job offers (https://www.consumerprotection.govt.nz/general-help/scamwatch/types-of-scams).

**Licensed advice.** INZ: "You are not required to use an adviser... only use a licensed immigration adviser (LIA), a certified New Zealand lawyer, or someone else legally able to provide immigration advice." (INZ scams URL above).

### Candidate lines

**why**
- A NZ-style CV is plain, tailored to each job ad and short, so it is easy to read and to pass through applicant tracking systems. https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv/
- Employers in NZ can legally offer a job before the candidate holds a work or resident visa. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/for-employers/rules-and-laws-when-hiring-people-from-overseas/rules-for-offering-a-job-to-a-person-from-overseas/

**steps**
- Open Tahatū's "How to write a CV" page and download the template and example that match your situation (skills-focused or work-focused). https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv/
- Write your CV in plain black font such as Calibri 11, with bullet points and short sentences, and without images. Tahatū's own words: "don't use images". https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv/
- Put your name, contact details, a personal statement, key skills, work history (most recent first) and education on it. https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv/
- Keep it to 2 pages if you can (Tahatū CV guide); Tahatū's workbook checklist says no more than two to three pages. Figure 1: 2 pages, B, quote "keep your CV to 2 pages if you can", https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv/. Figure 2: 2 to 3 pages, B, quote "CV is no more than two to three pages.", https://tahatu.govt.nz/api/documents/serve/233/Applying_for_jobs_CVs_cover_letters_and_more_workbook.pdf
- Reword your CV for each ad using its keywords and sentences. https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv/
- Write a cover letter of 1 page that shows how your skills and experience match the job; explain big gaps in work history there. Figure: 1 page, B, quote "keep your cover letter to 1 page", https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cover-letter/
- Search employment websites, recruitment companies and sector sites, and contact employers directly. INZ calls direct approaches "an accepted and often successful approach". https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/finding-work-in-new-zealand/how-to-find-a-job-in-new-zealand/
- Follow each ad's application instructions exactly, send a PDF unless a Word file is asked for, and check the application was sent and received. https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/job-application-checklist/
- Before you accept a role, read the written employment agreement and ask about anything unclear. https://www.employment.govt.nz/starting-employment/employment-agreements/creating-an-employment-agreement
- If your partner will look for work: Check the conditions on their visa in their eVisa and read INZ's Partner of a Worker Work Visa page before they apply for jobs. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/requirements-for-work-visas/check-or-change-your-work-visa-conditions/

**documents**
- Tahatū CV template and example (.docx) for your CV type. https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv/
- A PDF of your CV named with your first and last name and "CV", for example sam-henderson-CV.pdf, and a Word copy ready if an employer asks. https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv/
- A list of 2 or more referees with contact details, who know to expect a call. Figure: at least 2, B, quote "You'll need contact details for at least 2", https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv/
- A written offer and employment agreement; an offer made before your visa is held can say "Subject to immigration criteria being met". https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/for-employers/rules-and-laws-when-hiring-people-from-overseas/rules-for-offering-a-job-to-a-person-from-overseas/

**mistakes**
- Sending the same CV to every employer instead of changing it to each ad's wording. https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv/
- Adding images, text boxes or tables, which Tahatū's workbook checklist says to remove. https://tahatu.govt.nz/api/documents/serve/233/Applying_for_jobs_CVs_cover_letters_and_more_workbook.pdf
- Listing referees without telling them first. https://tahatu.govt.nz/api/documents/serve/159/Job_Hunters_Workbook__Web_version.pdf
- Using the old careers.govt.nz CV Builder or links to it; that site now redirects to Tahatū. https://www.careers.govt.nz/tools/

**watchOuts**
- Tahatū says CV builders may charge or hide costs and may store data abroad; it says not to share "sensitive personal data, such as bank account numbers or passwords". https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv/
- Depending on your job, you may need to register with a professional body to get a job and a visa. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/finding-work-in-new-zealand/how-to-find-a-job-in-new-zealand/
- Offers that ask you to pay a "processing" or "placement" fee conflict with INZ's statement that employers cannot charge for a job. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/protecting-yourself-from-immigration-scams/
- Own Your Online lists as red flags a job on a single board, a quick move to instant messaging, and an out-of-the-blue job offer. https://www.ownyouronline.govt.nz/personal/get-protected/guides/protect-yourself-from-job-scams/
- A genuine employer won't need your bank account details until you have accepted a job, per Own Your Online. https://www.ownyouronline.govt.nz/personal/get-protected/guides/protect-yourself-from-job-scams/
- Employment NZ says it is unlawful to ask a job applicant questions that indicate an intention to discriminate on grounds including age and marital or family status. https://www.employment.govt.nz/resolving-problems/bullying-harassment-and-discrimination/discrimination
- If your partner will look for work: Their visa conditions can limit who they work for, the occupation and the location, so they should check the eVisa first. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/requirements-for-work-visas/check-or-change-your-work-visa-conditions/

**cost**
- INZ: "You do not need to pay the company or agency as they get paid by the employer when they place someone in a job." https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/finding-work-in-new-zealand/how-to-find-a-job-in-new-zealand/
- Applying for an NZ job costs no fee to the employer or recruiter; INZ says it "only charges you an application fee" for visas. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/protecting-yourself-from-immigration-scams/
- If your partner will look for work: INZ lists the Partner of a Worker Work Visa from a fee of NZD $1630. Figure: 1630, A, quote "From NZD $1630", https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/partner-of-a-worker-work-visa/ (confirm on the page before relying on it).

**advice**
- INZ says you are not required to use an adviser; if you do, use a licensed immigration adviser, a certified NZ lawyer or someone else legally able to give immigration advice. https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/protecting-yourself-from-immigration-scams/
- Employment NZ does not give immigration advice; visa questions go to INZ. https://www.employment.govt.nz/resolving-problems/migrant-exploitation
- You can get professional advice on an employment agreement before you agree to take the job. https://www.employment.govt.nz/starting-employment/employment-agreements/creating-an-employment-agreement
