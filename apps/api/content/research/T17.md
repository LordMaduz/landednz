# T17 Check whether you need a qualification assessment (IQA) — research note

## Questions

- What is an International Qualification Assessment (IQA), and who may need one (visa, regulator, employer or salary)?
- Which qualifications does INZ list as not needing one, and can INZ still ask?
- What can and cannot NZQA assess, and is another country's evaluation accepted?
- Does a registration body's own assessment replace an IQA?
- How does the online application work, which documents are needed, and how are they verified?
- What does each IQA type cost, and how long does it take as NZQA states it?
- What is different for overseas-trained teachers (Teaching IQA)?

## Findings

**What an IQA is and who may need one.** INZ: "You may need to get an International Qualification Assessment (IQA) to show that your qualifications can be used in New Zealand." For visas that require a qualification to be "equivalent to" or "comparable with" a New Zealand one, "you will normally need to get an [IQA]. ... Most qualifications from outside New Zealand require an IQA." (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/requirements-for-work-visas/green-list-occupations-qualifications-and-skills/check-if-you-need-an-international-qualification-assessment/). NZQA says the IQA "allows individuals, employers and organisations to see how qualified a person is for work and immigration purposes." (https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/international/recognise-overseas-qual/iqa/). Its employer article adds: "The main reason people get an IQA is to meet immigration requirements, so they can live, work or study in New Zealand." (https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/about-us/news/understanding-international-qualification-assessments-iqa-for-new-zealand-employers/).

**Exemptions.** INZ: qualifications that do not need an IQA for visa applications are "engineering degrees accredited by the [Washington Accord / Sydney Accord signatories]" or "qualifications on the list of exemptions for the Skilled Migrant Category Resident Visa (SMC) — use the tool on this page to check the list." It adds: "Even if your qualification is on the list of exemptions, Immigration New Zealand may still ask you to get an IQA when we process your application." (INZ page above).

**Types.** INZ lists "skill shortage list IQA — for Green List jobs", "standard IQA — for comparing your qualifications with New Zealand ones, or to get occupational registration", and "teaching IQA — to register as a teacher or to get a teacher salary assessment." "You will need to include the results in your visa application." (INZ page above).

**What NZQA does and does not assess.** It can assess "a tertiary or vocational upper-secondary qualification ... a minimum of 400 notional learning hours (approximately 10 weeks of full time academic study)". It cannot assess "secondary school qualifications", "incomplete programmes of study", "letters or certificates of work experience", or "professional registration, permits, licenses or certifications". "We cannot evaluate incomplete applications. If you have not completed your qualification, or you do not have the required evidence, do not apply." "We cannot accept recognition evaluations from other countries ... Before applying for an IQA you could ask the person or organisation you are applying to whether they will accept a qualification evaluation from another country." (https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/international/recognise-overseas-qual/iqa/). "NZQA does not consider an individual's work experience or competency in its evaluation. This is for the relevant professional registration or licensing body, or employer to consider." (employer article above). The result is "a recognition statement. This tells you where the qualification fits within the New Zealand Qualifications and Credentials Framework." (https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/international/recognise-overseas-qual/apply-iqa/).

**Registration bodies.** No official page found states in general whether a regulator's assessment removes the need for an IQA. By regulator: the Nursing Council says "assessment of equivalency to New Zealand nursing qualifications will be made by the Nursing Council of New Zealand" and mentions no NZQA IQA on the pages read (https://www.nursingcouncil.org.nz/IQN?WebsiteKey=fa279da8-a3b1-4dad-94af-2a67fe08c81b). The PGDB says "You will need to submit an Overseas Qualification Pathway Application and evidence" to assess overseas qualification and work experience (https://www.pgdb.co.nz/apply_for_registration/register_with_overseas_qualifications/). NZQA notes that for a regulated profession such as teaching, an IQA gives assurance for the registration body (employer article above).

**How to apply.** NZQA: "Only submit your application when you have all the required documents." Steps are to confirm the IQA type, prepare documents, read terms ("Once you make an application there are usually no refunds"), check the fee ("Make sure you have a credit card ready for payment"), and apply through the NZQA portal ("Create an account"). Status is checked in the same portal. "We may cancel your application if we do not hear back from you within 15 days of contacting you." (apply page above).

**Documents.** NZQA video transcript: "we will need the photo and personal information page of your Passport", "all your post-secondary education award certificates", "all your transcripts, academic records or mark sheets for each year of post-secondary education"; name-change evidence if names differ ("marriage certificate, a deed poll document or an affidavit"); "all application documents are full colour scans of the original documents. We cannot accept black and white copies."; "If your original documents are not in English, you must provide an English translation by a recognised official translation service" (https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/international/recognise-overseas-qual/iqa/requirements-for-evidence-and-document/). Verification: "You must provide an acceptable form of verification for your documents. If your documents cannot be verified, your application is incomplete. Do not apply unless you can meet the verification requirements on this page." Accepted routes include "your institution's or government authority's online platform", "direct verification from your awarding organisation" (not a forwarded email), or "a trusted and independent qualifications verification service". "please do not arrange for physical copies of your award documents to be sent to NZQA." "You may have to pay for the verification." (https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/international/recognise-overseas-qual/iqa/requirements-for-evidence-and-document/verify-documents-for-iqa/). On work experience: "do not submit evidence of work experience with your IQA application" (NZQA evaluating page above).

**Fees (NZ$, "All fees include GST (where applicable) and are subject to change").** Standard IQA NZ$445 ("for the evaluation of one qualification"); Teaching IQA NZ$746 ("This package includes evaluation of an additional 2 free Standard IQAs"); Teaching assessment add-on NZ$301 ("in addition to the previously paid IQA fee", within 12 months); pre-1998 qualification NZ$275 extra; appeal NZ$765 within "20 working days from the date your recognition outcome statement was issued"; "There is no refund if you choose to cancel your application."; "You may also be required to pay services fees by third parties" (https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/international/recognise-overseas-qual/apply-iqa/fees/).

**Time as NZQA states it.** "Due to high demand, the average wait times for IQA applications are now 10 weeks. This does not include weekends or public holidays." and "Once we have confirmed that the application you've submitted contains all the evidence and documentation we need, we complete 90 per cent of applications within 3 weeks (15 business days)." Delays occur if "your application is incomplete" or "there are issues with verifying your documents." (apply page above). The two statements are on the same page; both are quoted, neither is preferred.

**Teachers (EDUCATION).** NZQA: "If you completed a teaching qualification outside New Zealand, you need to apply for a Teaching International Qualification Assessment (Teaching IQA) to register as a teacher in New Zealand." The main teaching qualification goes in as the Teaching IQA and extras as "separate Standard IQA applications" (https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/international/recognise-overseas-qual/iqa/for-teachers/). Teaching Council: "Before you register with the Teaching Council, you need to apply for a Teaching IQA to register as a teacher in New Zealand." and "NZQA no longer pre-approves overseas teaching qualifications. ... An IQA may also be needed for other purposes, like setting your salary." For UK QTS and US state licensure, which NZQA cannot assess, "you can still apply. The Teaching Council may make an exception" (https://teachingcouncil.nz/en/become-a-teacher/overseas-trained-teacher-coming-to-Aotearoa/requirements-to-teach-in-nz). NZQA and the Teaching Council ask for different document forms (NZQA: colour scans plus verification; Teaching Council: certified copies of the IQA report and qualifications); they are separate steps for separate bodies.
