## Findings

**Reporting scam texts.** DIA: "Forward the spam or scam message from your phone free of charge to our shortcode: 7726 . You will receive a TXT response from us (the Department of Internal Affairs) asking you to complete your report." (https://www.dia.govt.nz/Spam-Report-TXT-Spam). The reply step is optional: "Your report will still be registered even if you do not reply to us with the sender number of the spam or scam message you received." The page also names a built-in route: "You may report a suspicious message using the 'Report Junk' or 'Report Spam' (or equivalent wording) feature in your iOS messages app or Google Messages app". A report sent by mistake: "you can request its deletion by emailing info@antispam.govt.nz".

**Spotting scams.** Consumer Protection: "Have you been contacted unexpectedly by phone, text, email, social media, messaging apps or online? If someone contacts you out of the blue ... always consider the possibility that it may be a scam." It lists what scammers ask for: "personal or financial information, passwords, one-time verification codes, banking details, gift card codes, cryptocurrency payments, or access to your device or accounts" (https://www.consumerprotection.govt.nz/general-help/scamwatch/types-of-scams). On the "Hi Mum" scam: "The scammer will claim they have lost or damaged their phone and, as consequence, is making contact from a new number." The advice is to "call or contact your relative on the number already stored in your phone to check if it's real" (same page).

**If scammed.** Consumer Protection: "Stop all contact with the scammer ... Hang up the phone." and "contact your bank immediately". It points to "Report it (external link) — National Cyber Security Centre" (https://www.consumerprotection.govt.nz/general-help/scamwatch/scammed-take-action). The NCSC page lists "Individuals and small business — Report an online issue" (https://www.ncsc.govt.nz/report/). No NCSC phone number was captured.

**Emergency 111 on a mobile.** Police: "111 calls are free. You can call this number on a mobile phone even if the phone is out of credit." (https://www.police.govt.nz/call-111). The new-arrivals page: "Calling 111 is free from public telephones and mobile phones. When you call 111 you will be asked which emergency service you want: Police, Fire or Ambulance." (https://www.police.govt.nz/advice/personal-community/new-arrivals/english/communicating). It adds "If you have difficulty hearing or talking on the phone, you can register for an emergency TXT service." and "Use the 105 non-emergency number". The call-111 page says: "If you can't decide if it's a real emergency and you're still worried, call 111 and ask the operator." On location: "For calls made to 111 on a mobile cellular network, Police Emergency receives Emergency Caller Location Information (ECLI)." (https://www.police.govt.nz/contact-us/calling-emergency-111). govt.nz: "Not safe to talk? Stay on the line." (https://www.govt.nz/browse/law-crime-and-justice/crimes-and-emergencies/111-emergency-service/).

**Keeping a number.** Commerce Commission: "Number portability enables a customer to switch telecommunications providers while keeping the same phone number." and "Local and mobile number portability (LMNP) are both regulated under the Telecommunications Act." (https://www.comcom.govt.nz/regulated-industries/telecommunications/regulated-services/copper-services/local-and-mobile-number-portability/). The page also says "The Determination [2021] came into force on 20 December 2021." Whether it was renewed is unconfirmed.

**Complaints.** govt.nz: "If you have an issue with your electricity, gas, water, internet or phone provider, you have the right to complain and have the issue resolved." Step one: "Contact the provider directly to make a complaint." Then: "Internet and phone — Telecommunications Dispute Resolution ( TDR ) ." and "These services are free and independent." (https://www.govt.nz/browse/consumer-rights-and-credit-record/how-to-complain/complain-about-your-utility-provider/, last updated 05 August 2025).

**Not found.** No opened official page covers INZ phone guidance, ID to buy a SIM, 3G dates or cost. See Uncertainties.
