## Findings
- Rule: arrivals before the change date get 18 months, arrivals on or after get 12 months, per NZTA (facts nzta.licence-*).
- At period end: convert or stop driving. Allow plenty of time; test bookings may be slow.
- Valid for conversion: current or expired within 12 months, not suspended/disqualified/revoked in issuing country. IDP alone is not enough; physical licence required.
- Exempt countries have similar licensing systems; any other is non-exempt. Convert at a specialist overseas conversion agent.
- Translation: needed when driving and converting if not in English; authorised service, diplomatic representative, or issuing authority; IDP or China notary document may also be acceptable.

### 25 January 2027 vs 1 November 2026

"25 January 2027" appears only on the exempt and non-exempt pages: "The Government has announced changes to the car graduated driver licensing system from 25 January 2027. This does not change the way you convert your overseas car licence to a New Zealand licence." It is tied to the graduated driver licensing changes only, not to the overseas-licence time limit. It sits below a link labelled "Time limit change for driving on an overseas licence", but the sentence itself is about GDLS. The time-limit page, converting-car and index pages do not mention 25 January 2027. The time-limit date stays 1 November 2026 ("The extension … will end on 31 October 2026"). No conflict with nzta.licence-change-date. No T16 line uses 25 January 2027, which is correct.
