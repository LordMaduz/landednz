# LandedNZ — User Personas v2
*Revised: February 2026*
*Based on MBIE 2023 Migrant Survey (n=5,318), OECD 2024 Migration Outlook, Immigration NZ visa statistics*

---

## Segmentation Framework

Personas are organised by **life stage + settlement complexity**, not visa type. Visa type is a product variable handled at onboarding, not a market segment. The differentiating factor is the number of lives being set up simultaneously and the degree of interdependency between those lives.

| Segment | Complexity Multiplier | MVP Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Constrained Couple | 2x — two lives, one with restricted work rights | **Primary** |
| Family with Children | 3x+ — two adults + dependent school-age lives | Secondary |
| Regulated Professional | 1.5x — one life, but credential barrier adds major blocker | Post-MVP |

## Persona 1 — Priya & Karthik (PRIMARY)
### The Constrained Couple

---

### Who They Are

**Priya**, 28. Arrived on a Partner of Student Work Visa. Her husband Karthik holds a student visa studying a Level 8 Master's in Information Technology at Auckland University of Technology — a Green List qualification. This entitles Priya to open work rights under the Partner of Student Work Visa scheme introduced mid-2024.

**Karthik**, 30. Primary visa holder. Full-time student. Limited ability to work beyond 20 hours per week during term time. His study schedule determines where they live, when Priya needs to be available, and what the couple's financial ceiling is for the next 18–24 months.

They arrived together. No children. Came from Hyderabad with moderate savings, family support back home, and a clear plan: Karthik finishes his degree, both build NZ work history, then apply for residency via the Green List pathway.

The plan looks logical from India. It looks very different on the ground in Auckland.

---

### The Situation That Makes Them Unique

Priya and Karthik's settlement is **structurally interdependent**. Neither is settling solo. But they are also not a standard couple where both can simply go find jobs and get on with it. One person's visa, schedule, and status shapes the ceiling of what the other can do.

This creates a specific category of problems that neither generic "migrant advice" sites nor solo-settler content addresses:

- Priya needs income immediately. Karthik cannot fully contribute. The pressure lands on her.
- Priya has no NZ work history, no NZ references, and no network. She is job-seeking cold.
- Karthik cannot freely optimise for income — he is constrained to campus proximity and term schedules.
- Their joint financial position (savings burn rate vs. single income) determines how long they can sustain the plan without distress.
- If either of them makes a wrong financial decision early — wrong KiwiSaver election, wrong bank, emergency tax rate — the compounding effect on a single income is severe.

---

### Demographics Context

This persona is designed to represent, at the broadest level:

- **Partner of Student Work Visa holders** — partners of students studying Green List Level 7+ qualifications. Direct match.
- **Partner of AEWV holders** — where one partner holds full AEWV work rights and the other arrives on a dependent partner visa with conditional open work rights. Same structural dynamic: one anchors, one navigates entry-level.
- **Green List couples in early settlement** — both technically have work rights, but one is studying or in an early-stage role without stable income yet.

Combined realistic market at any time: approximately 50,000–80,000 people in active settlement. Structurally underserved by existing content which assumes either solo migration or symmetric dual-income couples.

---

### Pain Points — Ranked by Severity and Universality

#### Tier 1: Financial — Immediate and High Stakes

**IRD emergency tax rate**
Without a valid IRD number and tax code declaration submitted before the first paycheck, Priya's employer defaults her to the no-notification (emergency) tax rate of 45% on all income. On a $60,000 salary this costs approximately $800/month in overpayments during the months it takes to sort. On a single-income couple budget, this is catastrophic. The fix exists — IRD number application, tax code declaration to employer — but the sequence matters and the window is narrow.

*Why it hits harder here than other personas:* There is no second income to absorb the loss while the error is corrected. Karthik's 20-hour limit means no quick fix.

**KiwiSaver default trap**
Without active opt-out or rate selection, Priya is enrolled at 3% contribution by default, with employer contributing an additional 3%. On a tight budget, she may not want any KiwiSaver contribution in Year 1. Or she may want the minimum to capture the employer contribution as a long-term saving. But she makes no active choice because nobody explains the decision exists. She just notices her take-home is lower than expected.

*Downstream consequence:* If she leaves NZ permanently within 5 years without applying for a KiwiSaver withdrawal (possible under certain residency conditions), she may lose access to contributions made.

**Cost of living shock**
60% of migrants in MBIE 2023 survey rated cost of living as worse than expected. For Priya and Karthik, this hits harder than the survey average because they arrived with a budget built on Indian cost references. Auckland rent alone consumes 40–50% of a single moderate income. Groceries, transport, and utilities run 2–3x Hyderabad comparisons. Their financial model built before arrival is wrong by approximately 30–40% and they discover this in the first 4–6 weeks.

**Remittance fees**
Both Priya and Karthik send money home — for family support, loan repayments on Karthik's tuition financing, and maintaining savings in INR. Using bank-to-bank international transfer: 3–5% total cost including exchange rate spread and fees. Wise or similar: 0.4–0.8%. On $500/month sent home, this is $130–$230/year in unnecessary cost. Not life-changing but compounds over 18–24 months.

---

#### Tier 2: Logistical — Time-Sensitive and Sequential

**Rental market speed and documentation requirements**
Auckland rental stock turns over within 24–72 hours of listing. Priya and Karthik are competing against applicants with NZ rental history, NZ employer references, and NZ bank statements. They have none of these. Their first application is likely to fail not because they are bad tenants but because they cannot produce what Property Managers require. Understanding what can substitute (overseas references, employment offer letters, larger bond offer) is not documented anywhere clearly.

**The bank-address circular dependency**
To open a bank account: need NZ address.
To secure NZ address (rental): need proof of income and ideally a bank account.
To receive income: need a bank account.

This loop is real and catches almost every new arrival. It is solvable — banks accept certain overseas documents during a temporary grace period, and some accept hostel/temporary accommodation addresses — but only if you know the sequence. Priya and Karthik don't know the sequence.

**IRD number timing**
Cannot submit tax code declaration to employer without an IRD number. IRD number application requires NZ address and bank account (or can be done with overseas passport and temporary address in some cases). Processing takes 8–15 working days. If Priya starts work before this is resolved, she defaults to emergency tax. The sequence: temporary accommodation address → bank account → IRD application → IRD number → tax code declaration → submit to employer before first paycheck.

**Karthik's work rights management**
During standard term time, Karthik can work maximum 20 hours per week. During scheduled breaks, he can work full-time. He needs to track term calendars against his employment to ensure he does not accidentally breach his visa conditions — an error with serious downstream consequences for their residency pathway.

---

#### Tier 3: Social and Psychological — Slow Burn but High Churn Risk

**Social isolation as a unit**
Priya and Karthik have each other, which is both an asset and a risk. They are not acutely lonely in the way a solo migrant is, but they are an insular unit in a new country with no external network. Without deliberate effort to build connections outside the couple, they risk staying in a bubble — functional but not integrated. This matters because:
- Professional networks are how Priya gets her second job and her referees for future visa applications.
- Social networks are how they learn informal knowledge (which GP is good, which suburb to avoid, how NZ workplaces actually operate).
- Integration quality affects both their mental health over a 2–3 year settlement horizon.

MBIE data shows social connectedness is the strongest predictor of long-term settlement satisfaction, above income and employment.

**Workplace culture adjustment (Priya)**
NZ workplace culture is flat, collaborative, and places high value on proactive communication and "speaking up." For someone from a more hierarchical workplace culture, the unwritten rules are invisible. Priya may under-communicate with her manager, wait to be asked for input, and be perceived as disengaged — when she is in fact being appropriately deferential by her own cultural framework. This costs her in performance reviews and slows career progression.

**Healthcare navigation**
30% of migrants in MBIE 2023 survey rated healthcare as worse than expected. For Priya and Karthik, the key confusion points are: GP registration (you cannot simply show up; you need to enroll, and practices in Auckland are often at capacity), after-hours options (not equivalent to emergency care), ACC (covers injury regardless of visa status, free), and the difference between what is publicly funded and what requires out-of-pocket payment.

---

### Journey Map — First 90 Days

**Week 1–2: Survival Mode**
Temporary accommodation. Airport, exhaustion, SIM card, orientation. First attempt to open a bank account — confusion about what documents are accepted. Hunting for short-term rental while staying in an Airbnb bleeding savings. Priya applies for jobs simultaneously while physically homeless.

**Week 3–4: The Paperwork Sprint**
Rental found (took 3 attempts, final success required overseas reference letters and a larger bond offer). Bank account opened. IRD application submitted — 8-15 working days for number. Priya's job offer accepted but start date is in 2 weeks. Race to get IRD number before first paycheck. Tax code declaration submitted — just in time, or not.

**Week 5–8: First Income, First Shocks**
First paycheck arrives. Either correct (if IRD sequence worked) or 45% taxed (if it didn't). KiwiSaver deduction noticed but not understood. Grocery bill shock. First month's rent. Savings burn rate much higher than planned. Karthik's semester begins; his schedule constrains evening options, social flexibility, and additional income.

**Week 9–12: Stabilisation or Stress Fracture**
If financial setup was handled correctly and Priya's income is intact, couple begins stabilising. Social isolation sets in — the practical problems are solved but there are no friends, no weekend plans, no sense of belonging yet. If financial setup had errors (emergency tax, wrong KiwiSaver, poor remittance choices), this period is financial distress. Risk of early departure or poor decision-making under stress.

---

### What Priya & Karthik Need from LandedNZ

**Not another information dump.** They can Google. They need to know what to do, in what order, by when — with the specific context of their visa situation and financial constraints. They need the IRD sequence explained in steps. They need to know KiwiSaver is a decision, not a default. They need to know Wise exists before they wire money from their bank.

**Sequenced, personalised roadmap.** The order of setup tasks matters. Getting it wrong compounds. LandedNZ's value is: given your visa type, your arrival date, and whether you have a job offer yet — here is your exact sequence for the next 30 days.

**Dual-partner awareness.** Generic migrant tools treat households as individuals. Priya and Karthik have joint finances, interdependent visa conditions, and shared logistical problems. LandedNZ should recognise them as a unit — not two separate users accidentally sharing a postcode.

**Honest financial calibration.** Not cheerleading. Tell them what Auckland genuinely costs. Help them audit their pre-arrival budget against reality. Surface the decisions they are making passively (KiwiSaver, emergency tax, bank fees) before the cost hits them.

---

### Motivations

- Execute the plan: Karthik finishes degree → both build NZ work history → residency application
- Protect savings during study period — every unnecessary financial loss delays the plan
- Priya wants career continuity, not just employment — she wants a role that progresses, not just income
- Both want to feel they made a smart decision to come to NZ, not a naive one

### Fears

- Running out of money before Karthik graduates
- Making an immigration mistake that jeopardises the residency pathway
- Priya's career stalling at entry level because she doesn't understand NZ workplace culture
- Feeling permanently like outsiders — functional but not belonging

## Persona 2 — Aroha & Raj (SECONDARY)
### The Family with Children

---

### Who They Are

**Raj**, 35. Software engineer. Arrived on AEWV under the Green List (Tier 2 — shortage occupation). Had a job offer before landing. Wife Aroha (born in NZ, met Raj while working overseas) returned with him. Two children: 7 and 4.

**Context:** Raj's visa status is strong — Green List AEWV with a clear residency pathway. Aroha is a NZ citizen managing re-entry to the NZ job market after years abroad. Children need school enrollment immediately.

This persona is used here to represent the highest-complexity family settlement. The pain points are different from Persona 1: financial urgency is lower, but logistical complexity and the stakes of getting location decisions wrong are much higher.

---

### Unique Pain Point: The Suburb Decision Cannot Be Undone Cheaply

For Priya and Karthik, a wrong suburb choice costs them moving fees. For Raj and Aroha, a wrong suburb choice means the wrong school zone, which affects their 7-year-old's Year 2 enrollment and potentially requires a school zone application process that can take months — or a second move.

This is why school zone content belongs in Persona 2, not in top-level positioning.

**Other family-specific pain points:**
- School enrollment timeline and zone verification
- GP registration for children (Auckland pediatric practices often have waitlists)
- After-school care availability and cost
- Children's social integration — often harder than adult integration
- Raj managing work performance expectations while family setup is incomplete
- Dual-income restoration: Aroha re-entering NZ job market without recent NZ references despite being a citizen

---

### What They Need from LandedNZ

A roadmap that explicitly accounts for child-related timelines — school enrollment deadlines, pediatric GP registration, childcare options — layered on top of the standard adult setup sequence. The key value is: LandedNZ tells them which tasks must happen before they choose a suburb, so the suburb decision is made with full information.

## Persona 3 — Maria (POST-MVP SIGNAL)
### The Regulated Professional

---

### Who She Is

**Maria**, 32. Registered Nurse in the Philippines. Arrived on a Green List Tier 1 visa (straight-to-residence eligible occupation). Partner stayed in Manila pending Maria's settlement.

Her core blocker is not financial setup or rental — it is credential recognition. NZQA assessment and Nursing Council of NZ registration takes 10–16 weeks minimum. Until registered, she cannot work as a nurse. She is paying Auckland rent on depleted savings or a temporary non-nursing job while waiting for paperwork she cannot accelerate.

---

### Why She's Post-MVP

She's real, she's numerous (healthcare is the largest Green List category), and she has acute unmet needs. But her core problem — credential recognition delay — is not solvable by LandedNZ at MVP. It requires direct integration with NZQA and professional bodies, which is a B2B and regulatory play, not a consumer product feature.

**The post-MVP signal:** If LandedNZ validates with Personas 1 and 2, Maria represents the B2B expansion — employer partnerships with DHBs, private hospitals, and healthcare groups who sponsor Green List nurses and would pay for pre-arrival onboarding tools. This is a different business model with significantly higher ARPU than consumer subscriptions.

---

## What Is NOT a Persona (Deliberately Excluded from MVP)

**Working Holiday Maker** — 32,000+ arrivals annually, under 30, temporary by design, max 12 months. Different product entirely: travel-adjacent, gig-income focused, no settlement intent. Serves as noise if included in LandedNZ positioning. Exclude.

**Parent visa holders** — Small volume, older demographic, distinct financial situation, distinct support needs. Out of scope for MVP.

**Refugees and humanitarian entrants** — Served by government resettlement programs. Different channel, different needs, different funding model. Out of scope.
