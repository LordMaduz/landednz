# 01 — NZ Problem Landscape Research
*Research conducted: February 2026*
*Purpose: Broad sweep of NZ problems and opportunity areas before candidate selection*

---

## RESEARCH OBJECTIVE

Identify the most promising problem areas in New Zealand that:
- Have a clear software solution gap
- Affect enough NZers that a product has real user potential
- Can be built as an MVP in 4–6 weeks
- Are enhanced by AI in a meaningful way
- Ideally connect to founder's background for credibility

---

## NZ ECONOMIC CONTEXT (2025)

- NZ real GDP contracted 0.5% in 2024, recovering with projected 1.4% growth in 2025
- Unemployment at 5.1% in December 2024 quarter — highest since September 2020
- IT sector projected to grow from NZD $7.12B (2025) to NZD $8.52B by 2029 (CAGR 4.57%)
- Economy recovering slowly — organisations prioritising "do more with less"
- Reserve Bank cut official cash rate 6 times since August 2024 (total 225 basis points)
- NZ productivity historically lags behind other advanced economies (OECD 2022)
- NZ Productivity Commission: country does "poor job of picking up technology quickly"

## SECTOR-BY-SECTOR PROBLEM SCAN

### 1. Technology & Digital Adoption

**Key findings:**
- Only 7% of NZ university graduates completed STEM degrees in 2023
- 12% increase in applications per tech job ad in 2024, yet hiring times lengthened 25%
- Skill gap widening in AI, cloud, cybersecurity, data science
- 88% of ANZ CIOs say cybersecurity remains top investment priority for 2025
- NZ businesses among slowest in Asia-Pacific to adopt digital tools
- Only 20% of organisations believe they have sufficient cybersecurity budget

**Opportunity signal:** Tech adoption gap is real but highly competitive space

---

### 2. SME Digitalisation

**Key findings:**
- 600,000+ businesses in NZ, 97% are small businesses (fewer than 20 employees)
- SMEs employ 31% of NZ's workforce
- Potential $8.6B GDP boost from accelerated SME digitalisation (Xero/NZIER report)
- Digital adoption "uneven and fragmented" — basic tools used widely, advanced tools rarely
- Only 31% of NZ SMEs have any written IT policy
- NZ businesses rank second-to-last out of 11 Asia-Pacific countries for engaging IT consultants
- 70% of NZ SMB leaders say keeping pace with technology is a challenge
- 50% say they lack time to properly learn new systems
- 82% of NZ SMBs experimenting with AI (above global average) — but adoption shallow
- Rising costs identified as biggest challenge for NZ SMEs in 2024

**Opportunity signal:** Real problem, huge market — but extremely crowded (Xero, MYOB, 
ServiceM8, Tradify, Fergus all competing). Hard to differentiate as new entrant.

---

### 3. Agriculture & AgriTech

**Key findings:**
- Agriculture generates ~80% of NZ export earnings
- NZ identified as one of best global proving grounds for agritech
- Key startups: Halter (GPS cow management), CropLogic (precision agriculture),
  Robotics Plus (farm automation), BioLumic (UV seed treatment)
- Labour shortage driving automation demand
- AI chatbot research underway at University of Canterbury/AgResearch —
  farmers lack trust in and access to AI advisory tools
- Small farmers and rural operations severely underserved by existing agritech
  (most solutions target large operations)
- BCG report: NZ can double agritech investment and become world's "living laboratory"
  for sustainable agricultural innovation by 2050

**Opportunity signal:** Real gap for small farmers — but requires deep domain knowledge
founder doesn't have. Trust barrier extremely high (farmers make $1000s decisions on advice).
Long game requiring years of domain expertise. Not suitable for 6-week MVP.

---

### 4. Healthcare

**Key findings:**
- Healthcare professionals consistently on NZ Green List (critical shortage)
- AI tools helping doctors diagnose, serve remote areas, manage workloads (e.g. Volpara)
- Script Sense (NZ startup): AI platform reducing pharmacy medication errors
- Rural healthcare gap significant — geography creates access problems
- Mental health system under severe pressure
- Aged care sector facing workforce crisis

**Opportunity signal:** Real problems but heavily regulated. Clinical AI requires 
medical validation, compliance frameworks, and domain expertise far beyond MVP scope.

---

### 5. Trades & Construction

**Key findings:**
- NZ tradies (plumbers, electricians, builders) spend 10–15 hours/week on admin
- Lost admin time costs tradies thousands in lost earnings annually
- Existing tools: Tradify (60,000+ users globally, Kiwi-built), Fergus (Kiwi-built),
  Simpro, NextMinute, AroFlo, Zynoff (2025 launch)
- All existing tools focus on quoting, scheduling, invoicing
- None have meaningfully integrated AI voice-first interaction
- Market well-served but AI layer is genuine gap

**Opportunity signal:** AI differentiation possible but market crowded with trusted 
incumbents. Tradies notoriously resistant to new tech adoption. High trust barrier.

---

### 6. Migrant Settlement

**Key findings:**
- 130,000+ migrant arrivals annually (136,100 in October 2025 year)
- 29% of NZ population are immigrants (2023)
- Top source countries: India, Philippines, China, Sri Lanka
- 60%+ of Asian-born migrants concentrate in Auckland
- Government settlement resources: static webpages across multiple agencies
- Existing NGO support (Belong Aotearoa etc.): human-powered, geographically limited,
  cannot scale to 130,000 arrivals/year
- No AI-powered personalized settlement product exists for NZ
- Government's Migrant Settlement and Integration Strategy (refreshed 2023) defines
  success across 5 outcome areas: participation, health, housing, education, employment
- Accredited employers legally required to provide settlement info within 30 days —
  most don't or do it poorly
- Migrants navigate through Facebook groups and Reddit — unreliable, informal

**Opportunity signal:** STRONGEST of all sectors. Empty competitive space, large market,
clear AI differentiation, government priority area, founder IS the target user.

## CANDIDATE SHORTLIST

Based on broad research, 4 candidates identified for deeper evaluation:

| # | Name | Problem | AI Angle |
|---|------|---------|----------|
| 1 | SME Digital Advisor | NZ SMEs can't navigate digital transformation | AI audits business, recommends tool stack |
| 2 | Tradie AI Assistant | 10–15hrs/week admin burden for trade businesses | Voice-first AI: speak job details, get quote/invoice |
| 3 | NZ Migrant Settlement Companion | Fragmented, unsequenced settlement journey | Personalized AI roadmap by visa type, family, city |
| 4 | AI Farm Advisor | Small farmers data-rich but insight-poor | Conversational AI farm advisor in plain English |

---

## CANDIDATE ELIMINATION ANALYSIS

### Candidate 1 vs Candidate 3
- Candidate 1 (SME): Huge market but extremely crowded. Xero, MYOB, ServiceM8 
  have years of trust and deep integrations. Hard to differentiate as new entrant.
- Candidate 3 (Migrants): Empty competitive space. AI angle immediately executable.
  Founder is the user — authentic advantage.
- **Winner: Candidate 3**

### Candidate 2 vs Candidate 3
- Candidate 2 (Tradies): Market served (not perfectly). Tradify is Kiwi-built with 
  60K+ users. Tradies resistant to new tech. Voice-to-quote requires deep supplier 
  catalogue integration — not a 6-week MVP.
- Candidate 3 (Migrants): Less saturated, AI more immediately executable, 
  trust barrier lower — immigrants actively seek help and adopt quickly.
- **Winner: Candidate 3**

### Candidate 4 vs Candidate 3
- Candidate 4 (Farmers): Hardest technical problem. Requires domain-specific training 
  data. Founder has zero agriculture domain knowledge. Existing agritech startups with 
  PhDs and government funding still struggle with adoption.
- Candidate 3 (Migrants): Founder can build authentically — they are the user.
- **Winner: Candidate 3**

## FINAL SELECTION

**✅ Candidate 3 — NZ Migrant Settlement Companion (LandedNZ)**

### Selection Rationale
1. Empty competitive space — no AI-powered product exists
2. Large, growing market — 130,000+ arrivals/year, 29% of NZ population
3. AI changes the equation — personalization at scale was impossible before LLMs
4. Founder authenticity — Ruchira IS the target user (arriving Auckland mid-2026)
5. Government priority — aligns with NZ Migrant Settlement and Integration Strategy
6. MVP buildable in 6 weeks — web app first, no deep integrations required in v1
7. Clear monetisation path — freemium + B2B employer white-label expansion
