# 02 — Candidate Analysis & Selection
*Research conducted: February 2026*
*Purpose: Deep comparison of all 4 candidates with scoring and final selection rationale*

---

## EVALUATION FRAMEWORK

Every candidate evaluated against 7 filters:

1. **Problem clarity** — Is the problem well-defined and real?
2. **Market size** — Does it affect enough people to matter?
3. **Competitive gap** — Is there space for a new entrant?
4. **AI differentiation** — Does AI meaningfully change what's possible?
5. **Founder domain knowledge** — Does Ruchira have authentic credibility here?
6. **MVP buildability** — Can a working MVP be built in 4–6 weeks?
7. **Monetisation clarity** — Is there a clear path to revenue?

## CANDIDATE 1 — SME Digital Advisor

### Problem Statement
97% of NZ businesses are SMEs. They are among the slowest in Asia-Pacific to adopt 
digital tools — costing the economy an estimated $8.6B in lost GDP productivity.
SME owners know they need to digitise but face three walls: too many tool choices, 
no time to learn them, and no trusted advisor to guide them.

### Market Size
- 600,000+ businesses in NZ
- 97% are small businesses (fewer than 20 employees)
- 31% of NZ workforce employed by SMEs
- $8.6B potential GDP boost from accelerated digitalisation (Xero/NZIER 2025)

### Root Causes
- Too many competing tools with no clear guidance on which to choose
- 70% of NZ SMB leaders say keeping pace with technology is a challenge
- 50% say they lack time to properly learn new systems
- Only 31% of NZ SMEs have any written IT policy
- NZ ranks second-to-last in Asia-Pacific for engaging IT consultants

### Why Still Unsolved
- Government Digital Boost programme exists but is generic and non-personalised
- MSPs (Managed Service Providers) exist but are expensive for micro-businesses
- No AI-powered tool that audits a specific business and recommends a specific stack

### AI Angle
An AI-powered "digital advisor" that:
- Audits a business in a 5-minute questionnaire
- Recommends specific tools for their industry and size
- Generates a step-by-step implementation plan
- Monitors adoption and suggests next steps

### Competitive Landscape
**Very crowded:**
- Xero (NZ-born, millions of users, deep integrations)
- MYOB (established, large NZ market share)
- ServiceM8, Tradify, Fergus (trade-specific)
- Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace (dominant at SME level)
- Countless generic business advisory tools globally

### Founder Edge
Medium. Enterprise Java/Spring Boot background means understanding integration 
complexity. But no direct SME operator experience or domain credibility.

### Scorecard
| Factor | Score (1–5) | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| Problem clarity | 5 | Well-documented, data-rich |
| Market size | 5 | 600,000 SMEs, $8.6B problem |
| Competitive gap | 2 | Extremely crowded |
| AI differentiation | 3 | Possible but hard to execute uniquely |
| Founder domain knowledge | 3 | Transferable but not authentic |
| MVP buildability | 3 | Scope creep risk is high |
| Monetisation clarity | 4 | Clear but hard to charge SMEs |
| **TOTAL** | **25/35** | |

### Verdict
Strong problem, wrong battle. The market is a war zone with well-funded incumbents.
A solo MVP entering this space has no meaningful differentiation without years of 
trust-building and deep integrations. **Eliminated.**

## CANDIDATE 2 — Tradie AI Assistant

### Problem Statement
NZ trade business owners (plumbers, electricians, builders, landscapers) spend 
10–15 hours per week on admin tasks — quoting, invoicing, scheduling — mostly 
after hours. This lost time directly costs them thousands in lost earnings annually.

### Market Size
- Construction and trades is one of NZ's largest employment sectors
- Significant portion of 600,000 NZ SMEs are trade businesses
- Every tradie is a potential user — solo operators to teams of 10+
- Willingness to pay proven — Tradify, Fergus, Simpro all have paying customers

### Root Causes
- Tradies are skilled at their trade, not at business administration
- Quoting requires knowing material costs, labour rates, supplier pricing
- Scheduling involves coordination across multiple jobs and team members
- Invoicing delayed = cash flow problems
- No time or appetite to learn complex software

### Why Still Unsolved
- Existing tools solve the problem partially but require significant setup and learning
- None have meaningfully integrated AI for voice-first interaction
- Tradies resistant to adopting new tech — high inertia

### AI Angle
Voice-first AI tradie assistant:
- Speak job details → quote generated automatically
- Speak completion → invoice sent immediately
- AI understands NZ supplier pricing and material costs
- Zero screen time required on the job site

### Competitive Landscape
**Well served but AI gap exists:**
- Tradify (Kiwi-built, 60,000+ users globally, strong NZ presence)
- Fergus (Kiwi-built, strong NZ market)
- Simpro (enterprise-grade, complex)
- NextMinute (residential focus)
- Zynoff (2025 launch, similar AI angle)
- AroFlo, Jobber, ServiceM8

### Founder Edge
Medium. Mobile (iOS) + AI + simplicity is the right stack. But no tradie 
industry experience. Supplier catalogue integrations required for accurate 
quoting are a significant technical challenge beyond 6-week scope.

### Scorecard
| Factor | Score (1–5) | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| Problem clarity | 5 | Acute, daily, well-documented |
| Market size | 4 | Large NZ tradie population |
| Competitive gap | 3 | AI gap exists but incumbents strong |
| AI differentiation | 3 | Voice-first compelling but hard to execute accurately |
| Founder domain knowledge | 2 | No tradie industry experience |
| MVP buildability | 2 | Supplier integrations make accurate quoting hard |
| Monetisation clarity | 5 | Clear willingness to pay proven |
| **TOTAL** | **24/35** | |

### Verdict
Real pain, real market, but two critical problems: strong incumbents with deep 
trust, and voice-to-accurate-quote requires supplier catalogue integrations that 
are not feasible in 6-week MVP. **Eliminated.**

## CANDIDATE 3 — NZ Migrant Settlement Companion (LandedNZ)

### Problem Statement
130,000+ migrants arrive in New Zealand annually. They face a fragmented, 
unsequenced set of tasks across multiple government agencies with no personalized 
guidance. The information exists on government websites — but nobody tells migrants 
what order to do things in and why the order matters. Costly mistakes happen in 
the first weeks due to ignorance, not incompetence.

### Market Size
- 130,000+ migrant arrivals annually (136,100 in October 2025 year)
- 29% of NZ population are immigrants (2023 data)
- Top source countries: India, Philippines, China, Sri Lanka
- 60%+ of Asian-born migrants concentrate in Auckland
- Accredited employers (17,500+) legally required to provide settlement info —
  potential B2B market on top of B2C

### Root Causes
**Fragmentation:** Government services delivered by separate agencies 
(IRD, MBIE, ACC, MSD, local councils) with no unified entry point.

**Generic information:** One-size-fits-all resources don't account for visa type, 
family situation, occupation, or city.

**No sequencing:** Nobody tells migrants what to do first, what depends on what, 
or how long each step takes. This is the core problem.

**No AI layer:** Existing resources are static webpages, PDFs, and NGO programmes 
that cannot personalise or scale.

### Why Still Unsolved
- Government incentive is to provide information, not to optimise the user experience
- NGOs (Belong Aotearoa etc.) are human-powered and geographically limited
- No commercial product has targeted this space with AI
- Facebook groups and Reddit are the current "solution" — unreliable and unstructured

### AI Angle
Three things AI makes possible that weren't before:

1. **Personalisation at scale** — 5 onboarding questions generate a completely 
   unique settlement roadmap for each user
2. **Conversational navigation** — Ask any NZ settlement question in plain English, 
   get a direct contextual answer (not a Google search)
3. **Proactive nudging** — Track settlement progress and send timely reminders 
   before problems happen (e.g. "Your foreign driver's licence expires in 3 months")

### Competitive Landscape
**Essentially empty:**
- No AI-powered NZ settlement companion exists
- Government: static webpages, no AI, no personalisation
- Belong Aotearoa: NGO, human-powered, Auckland-only playgroups
- Immigration NZ: visa processing only, not settlement guidance
- Facebook groups / Reddit: informal, unreliable, no structure
- Generic relocation apps (not NZ-specific, not AI-powered)

### Founder Edge
**Highest of all 4 candidates:**
- Ruchira IS the target user — arriving Auckland on Partner of Student Work Visa mid-2026
- Sri Lankan tech professional — exact demographic of primary user base
- Will live every pain point personally during the build
- Fintech background (complex data → actionable output) maps directly to product architecture
- Authentic storytelling in interviews: "I built this because I lived it"

### Scorecard
| Factor | Score (1–5) | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| Problem clarity | 5 | Crystal clear, government-documented |
| Market size | 5 | 130,000+/year, 29% of NZ population |
| Competitive gap | 5 | No meaningful competitor exists |
| AI differentiation | 5 | Personalisation + conversation + proactive nudging |
| Founder domain knowledge | 5 | Founder IS the user |
| MVP buildability | 5 | Web app achievable in 6 weeks |
| Monetisation clarity | 4 | Freemium + B2B employer expansion |
| **TOTAL** | **34/35** | |

### Verdict
Strongest candidate across every dimension. Empty competitive space, large market,
clear AI differentiation, government priority alignment, and highest founder 
authenticity of all candidates. **Selected.**

## CANDIDATE 4 — AI Farm Advisor

### Problem Statement
NZ's agriculture sector generates ~80% of export earnings but farmers are 
data-rich and insight-poor. They collect weather, soil, and livestock data 
but lack accessible AI tools to turn it into actionable decisions. Small farmers 
and rural operations are almost entirely underserved by existing agritech.

### Market Size
- Agriculture is backbone of NZ economy (~80% of export earnings)
- Thousands of small and medium farm operations across NZ
- Existing agritech targets large operations — small farmers unserved
- Government and research institutions actively funding agritech

### Root Causes
- Agritech tools designed for large operations with dedicated tech staff
- Small farmers lack time, budget, and digital literacy for complex platforms
- LLM-based advisory tools not yet available in NZ agriculture context
- Farmers need to trust advice before acting — trust takes years to build

### Why Still Unsolved
- Domain knowledge required is deep and highly specialised
- NZ-specific: soil types, regional weather, biosecurity rules, livestock regulations
- Without accurate domain data, AI gives generic advice farmers ignore
- University of Canterbury research confirms farmers don't yet trust AI tools
- Existing agritech companies with PhDs and government funding still struggle 
  with farmer adoption

### AI Angle
Conversational AI farm advisor:
- Ask questions in plain English
- Get actionable recommendations based on farm data + local weather + NZ regulations
- Democratise agronomist-level advice for small farm operators

### Competitive Landscape
**Moderately served at high end:**
- Halter (GPS cow management, well-funded, Auckland-based)
- CropLogic (precision agriculture, data analytics)
- Robotics Plus (farm automation)
- AgriZero NZ (public-private joint venture)
- Multiple global agritech startups entering NZ market via Land x Launch programme

### Founder Edge
**Lowest of all 4 candidates:**
- Zero agriculture domain knowledge
- Gap between financial event processing and farm advisory is enormous
- Would spend months learning domain before building anything credible
- No authentic connection to the problem

### Scorecard
| Factor | Score (1–5) | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| Problem clarity | 4 | Real but complex to define precisely |
| Market size | 3 | Large economically but hard to reach as users |
| Competitive gap | 3 | Small farm gap exists but trust barrier extreme |
| AI differentiation | 3 | Compelling concept but technically very hard |
| Founder domain knowledge | 1 | Zero agriculture background |
| MVP buildability | 1 | Domain data requirements make 6-week MVP impossible |
| Monetisation clarity | 2 | Farmers slow to pay for software |
| **TOTAL** | **17/35** | |

### Verdict
Fascinating problem but wrong founder, wrong timeline, wrong domain. 
Requires years of domain expertise and farmer trust-building. 
Not suitable for 6-week MVP. **Eliminated.**
